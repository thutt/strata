ANTHROPOMORPHISM AUDIT OF THE MANUAL SOURCES

Date of the audit: 22 November 2026.  The rules are checked by
manual/checkeditorial.py (E30, E31 and E32, run by build.sh check-style) and
tested by tests/editorial.py.

THE RULES

  1. Software is a mechanical device.  A program, a panel, a box, a bar, a
     message line, TeX, LaTeX or a terminal does not say, ask, tell, want,
     know, try, decide, refuse, understand, remember, forget, think, expect,
     assume, prefer, like, help, see, notice, guess, hope, insist or learn.
     E30 reports these verbs after a software subject (the Editor, the
     Document Manager, the Printer, the Viewer, Strata, a program, a panel, a
     box, a bar, a script, a system, TeX, LaTeX, a terminal, a checker, the
     thesaurus, a server, a message line, a pop-up, a footer, a notice, a
     message, a banner, Setup, Preview, the Error Log, and the commands
     Quit, Macro, Retrieve, Delete, Save, Replace and Search) and after the
     bare pronoun it.
  2. A defect of the software is called a defect or an error, never a bug.
     E31 reports the word in any source, in the program font too.
  3. What may be written:
       shows, draws, writes, reads, stores, keeps, returns, starts, stops,
       lists, counts, sends, sets, opens, closes, prints, makes, reports,
       prompts for, offers, puts a question, rejects, determines, requires.
     A message line shows a text; a panel prompts for a value; a check
     rejects a name; a rule determines a result.  Where the subject would
     have to be a person, the sentence is written in the passive voice.
     The model of the owner: "If any is missing, nothing is installed", not
     "If any is missing, it installs nothing at all".
  4. Not covered by the rules: a document, a table or a field that "says"
     what it holds (the text says otherwise, the table says which); a person
     or a service as the subject (a postal service asks for a place).

THE PRONOUN

  The bare it for a program cannot be found by a pattern: its noun is
  somewhere in the sentence before.  The sentences in which it stood for a
  program (start, end, reading and writing, and the like) were found by
  hand and rewritten, in about 65 places.  Examples:

     The Editor reads the two keys when it starts and loads the macro set
     and the library before it reads the first key.
   =>  The Editor reads the two keys at start and loads the macro set and
     the library before the first key is read.

     Strata is built to be used over a remote login: it writes only what
     changed and asks nothing of the terminal that a text terminal does
     not have.
   =>  Strata is built to be used over a remote login: only what changed is
     written, and nothing is requested of the terminal that a text
     terminal does not have.

     The Document Manager asks whether the new document is structured or
     unstructured.
   =>  A prompt of the Document Manager offers the choice between a
     structured and an unstructured document.

     the panel says "Typesetting..."   =>  the panel shows "Typesetting..."
     the Editor refuses an equation    =>  an equation ... is rejected
     TeX wants them (the packages)     =>  TeX requires them

  About 660 places have it or they before a verb of any kind.  In nearly
  all of them it stands for a key, a field, a marker, a list, a panel, a
  file or a document ("it opens the checklist", "it starts empty", "it
  holds the text"): the sentence says what the item does, with a verb of
  the machine, and no person is needed.  The pronoun pass below looked at
  all of them.

THE PRONOUN PASS

  The rule E32 of manual/checkeditorial.py finds what a pattern can find:
  (1) it or they, in a sentence that has software for its subject or in
  the sentence after it, with a bare or modal verb of a person or of
  intention ("it would not help", "they say") or with allows, lets,
  offers, ignores, recognizes, warns, complains, cares, chooses, picks or
  insists; (2) software with a modal and such a verb ("a terminal cannot
  see", "LaTeX would refuse", "Strata does not know").  A pronoun that is
  the object of a verb ("remove it like any character") is not reported.
  The software subjects are those of E30.  The rule looks at every source
  of the product, like E30 and E31, and the file format sources are not
  shipped and are not rewritten.

  Allowed verbs for it and they: is, has, holds, shows, stands, begins,
  starts, ends, needs (a dependency), takes, makes, prints, opens, closes,
  keeps, replaces, finds, reads, writes, stores, counts, lists, sets, runs,
  uses, draws, moves, changes, removes, belongs, follows, covers, works.
  Not allowed: the verbs of E30, the same verbs after a modal, allows,
  lets, offers, ignores, recognizes, warns, complains, cares, chooses,
  picks, insists, and the adverbs silently, politely and helpfully.

  Hits of E32 in the sources at the base of this job (the file format
  sources, which are not shipped, had three more) and after the rewrite:

                                                  build directory is needed

  Left as it was: a few sentences in which it stands for a document ("it
  says what they see", manual/README.txt) or for a person who is not
  software; the allowed verbs above; and the quoted messages of the
  programs.

WHAT WAS NOT CHANGED

  - The messages of the programs that the manual quotes exactly were
    left in this part of the work; the part about the code (below)
    changed the five of them that gave a program the acts of a
    person, with the messages tables of the manual and the tests.
  - The sources of the Strata File Formats (strata-file-format*.src): the
    document is not shipped.
  - The Tutorial and the cards (cheat.src, mathcheat.src): E30 and E31 run
    over them and report nothing; their words were not rewritten.
  - A comment in tests/keymap.py and the developer documents of docs/ other
    than the six that the first part of the rule changed earlier.

THE CODE

  The rules of the manual also apply to the text that the code contains:
  the comments and the string literals of the C sources, the comments and
  the strings of the Python and shell scripts, the help text of the Editor
  and the README files.  tests/codestyle.py finds the hits of the same
  pattern as E30 (a verb of a person after a software subject or after the
  bare pronoun it) and the word bug, with the subjects that only the code has
  (lpinfo, a dictionary, a function, a routine, a parser, a handler, a module
  and a callback).  A document, a table, a field or the header of a file that
  "says" what it holds is not software: "the record PO of its header says so"
  stays.

  By verb: says 136, asks 37, refuses 24, tells 9, knows 8, decides 3,
  sees 3, wants 2, tries 2, forgets 1.  The word bug had no hit.

  Rewrites, the same as in the manual: says and tells became shows or
  reports (the message line shows why), asks became prompts for or requests,
  refuses became rejects, knows became holds or determines, decides became
  determines, wants became requires.

  The messages of the programs that changed (each is quoted in the manual and
  in a test, and both were changed with it):
     "LaTeX says: %s"                    "LaTeX reported: %s"
     "lpinfo says:"                      "lpinfo reported:"
     "The hyphenation dictionary says N words and has M"
                                         "... dictionary gives N words ..."
     "TeX refuses the equation (REASON), so it is printed as text."
                                         "TeX rejects the equation ..."
     "The picture data is larger than its header says"
                                         "... larger than the size in its
                                         header"
  The passive "was refused" and "is refused" of the log lines and of the
  messages stay: no actor is named, the manual uses them in the same way, and
  the word is part of the output "refused: N" of strprint and of the
  identifiers of the diagnostics (picture-refused and the like).

  Not found by the pattern and therefore not rewritten: the pronoun it whose
  noun is in an earlier sentence of a comment.  The clear cases were
  rewritten by reading.  The test cannot find the rest.

  The rules are tested by tests/codestyle.py, which runs in the unit tests
  (make test).  It proves with samples that must fail that every group of the
  pattern finds what it is for, and with samples that must pass that the
  words of the machine are accepted.
