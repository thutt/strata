This file says how the Editor draws the corners, the tees and the crossings
where border lines meet on the screen.  The code is border_row, border_line
and join_char in src/view.c; the tests are tests/joins.py (the cells) and
tests/xsmoke.py (the pixels in X11).

1. THE CELLS OF A ROW BETWEEN TWO BLOCKS
----------------------------------------
  A row between two blocks (the row above the first block, the row below the
  last block, and the separator row between two blocks) is a row of cells.
  Each cell has a horizontal line or has none, and the vertical lines of the
  block above (its side borders, which come to the cell from above) and of
  the block below (which go down from it).

  The horizontal line of a cell is, in this order:
    1. the top border of the block below or the bottom border of the block
       above, when a column of one of them has one at that cell (the
       heavier of the two when both have one);
    2. nothing, when the cell lies between the lines of two columns (the
       gap between two bordered columns stays clear);
    3. the RULE of the separator row, when the row carries one, from the
       left offset of the block to the right edge;
    4. nothing.
  The rule is therefore the horizontal line of every cell that has no
  border line of a block.  It is not replaced as a whole by the border lines
  of a block: it goes on to the right of a bordered column (and to the left
  of one that does not start at the left offset).

  The character of a cell comes from the four directions that have a line
  (up, down, left and right of the cell centre) and from the kinds of the
  lines (single, double; wide is drawn as bold double).  The table is
  join_cp437 in src/view.c; where the horizontal lines or the vertical lines
  of one cell are of two kinds, the heavier kind is used (code page 437 has
  no character for a single line joined to a double one on one axis).

2. THE SEAM BETWEEN A BORDERED COLUMN AND ONE WITHOUT BORDERS  (2026-11-19)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Before this rule the whole rule was dropped as soon as any block had a
  horizontal border line of its own on the row.  The column without borders
  then had no line in the joining, so the right side of the bordered column
  was drawn as a right tee, while the plain rule still ran on to the right
  of it on the screen.  The owner called it a defect: the rule crosses the
  side, so the seam is a crossing.

  Now the seam is a crossing (single, double or wide, with the heavier kind
  where they differ) wherever the rule goes on to the right of the side, and
  a tee where it does not (the last column).  Above the first block and
  below the last block there is no rule, so the corners stay corners.

  The cases that the tests cover (tests/joins.py, "seam" and the others):
    bordered next to none, single, double and wide: crossing at the seam;
    the corner above the first block is a corner;
    two bordered columns side by side: the cell between them stays clear;
    sides only: tees and crossings of the rule with the sides;
    no border at all: the rule stays a plain line.
  The pixel test (tests/xsmoke.py) checks that the four arms of the crossing
  at the seam meet the cells around it in the pixels, with no gap.

3. THE OTHER OUTPUTS
--------------------
  The ASCII fallback draws a plus sign for every corner, tee and crossing
  (STRATA_ASCII=1), the screen image export writes the same characters in
  UTF-8 and code page 437 and plus, hyphen, equals sign and bar in 7 bit
  ASCII and Latin-1.  They take the cells of the screen, so they agree.
