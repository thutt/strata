1. What the original did
========================

1.1 Maintain Dictionaries  [V]
------------------------------
The Document Manager has the entry Maintain Dictionaries (the fifth).  It
opens a panel with six operations, in this order:

    Convert Document to Spelling Dictionary
    Add Document to Spelling Dictionary
    Convert Spelling Dictionary to Document

    Convert Document to Hyphenation Dictionary
    Add Document to Hyphenation Dictionary
    Convert Hyphenation Dictionary to Document

Each opens a panel of three fields, for example Convert Document to Spelling
Dictionary: Working Directory (C:\MS\MSDATA\), Document Name and Spelling
Dictionary Name; Ins converts.  The menu remembers its last entry, so Home
goes to the first one.  The names take their extension from the operation:
.DOC for a document, .SPC for a spelling dictionary, .HYC for a hyphenation
dictionary.  A name that does not exist gives "Cannot find file (Document
Name: HY1.DOC)".

1.2 Settings that use a hyphenation dictionary  [H]
---------------------------------------------------
The panel Hyphenation Settings (reached from Optional Settings of Final
Print, Preview and Compare Document Versions) has: Hyphenation (a check list:
Automatic, Custom Dictionary, Soft Hyphens, None; Space selects), Custom
Hyphenation Dictionary (a name, .HYC assumed, F2 lists), Minimum, Maximum and
Preferred Space Widths (0 to 999, 100 is the space of the font), Adjust
Maximum in Narrow Columns? (never over 150 percent), Maximum Consecutive
Hyphenated Lines, Minimum Consecutive Unhyphenated Lines, Minimum
Characters in Word, Minimum Characters Before Hyphen and Minimum Characters
After Hyphen.  A soft hyphen is typed with Ctrl-Hyphen and shows as a small
triangle that points down.  Help topic 261: "Soft hyphen error - a word uses
soft hyphens incorrectly: leading or final hyphens or more than one in the
same place".  The setup of the Document Manager has Dictionary Language
(American English), "the language to be used by the spell checker and for
hyphenation".

1.3 The rules for a document that becomes a dictionary  [H][V]
--------------------------------------------------------------
Words are separated by a space, a tab or an end of line (help topic 277).
For a spelling dictionary a word in lower case is also added with an initial
capital; a word with a capital is kept as typed.  For a hyphenation
dictionary the word has a soft hyphen where it may be broken; a word with no
soft hyphen stops the word from being hyphenated; the words are lower case
(the original hyphenates every case of the word) and possessives are entered.


2. The spelling dictionary (.SPC)  [V]  DECODED
===============================================
A custom spelling dictionary is a PLAIN TEXT FILE: words, each ended by
CR LF, and the byte 1A (Ctrl-Z) at the end.  The words are in byte order
(so every word with a capital comes before every word in lower case), with
no duplicates.  Test: a document of the words zorbaflux, quimbleton,
Xanthippe, BLORF, it's and aa-bb became (bytes shown as text):

    Aa-bb CRLF BLORF CRLF It's CRLF Quimbleton CRLF Xanthippe CRLF
    Zorbaflux CRLF aa-bb CRLF it's CRLF quimbleton CRLF zorbaflux CRLF 1A

Add Document to Spelling Dictionary merges the words of a second document in
the same way (flumdoodle, zorbaflux and Mxyzptlk added Flumdoodle,
flumdoodle and Mxyzptlk and no second zorbaflux).  Convert Spelling
Dictionary to Document writes a document of ONE block with the words as
lines (each ended by the byte 1D), in order without regard to case, a lower
case word before the same word with an initial capital, and a header record
MD "Listing of dictionary T1.SPC, 18-Nov-DATE".  The main dictionary
AMERICAN.SPL cannot be changed or converted (the manual says so).

This is the same kind of file as the personal word list that Strata keeps (the
file dictionary of the settings folder): one word to a line, the same capital
rule.


3. The hyphenation dictionary (.HYC)  [V]  DECODED
==================================================
A custom hyphenation dictionary is a file of PAGES OF 512 BYTES.  Page 0 is
the index; pages 1 and up hold the words.  All numbers are big endian.  The
layout is checked against 300 random words of two to four syllables (pages 1
to 7 of a file of 4096 bytes) and against the small files below: every word
and every hyphen position was read back exactly (tools/hyc.py).

Page 0 (the index):
    - 4-byte keys: key i is the first four letters of the first word of data
      page i+1; the first key of a one page file of the words backhoe to
      zorbaflux's is "back".
    - the rest of the keys are FF FF FF FF (unused).
    - the last four bytes are the number of words of the whole file.

A data page (1 to n):
    - the entries one after the other from the start of the page, in byte
      order of the word; the same word is not twice;
    - an entry is the word in LOWER case (a capital typed in the document
      becomes lower case), then a 4-byte mask;
    - the mask has bit i (counting from 0 as the lowest bit) set when the word
      may be broken after the letter i+1: bulldozer (bull-dozer) has 08,
      project (pro-ject) 04, quimbleton (quim-ble-ton) 48 (hex), zorbaflux
      (zor-ba-flux) 14, a word with no hyphen 00000000;
    - the unused part of the page is FF;
    - at the end of the page: one byte for each entry with the length of the
      entry in bytes (word plus 4), in the order of the entries, and then
      the last byte of the page, the number of entries of the page.
      Test: a page of the 6 words had the trailer 0B 0D 0B 0E 0D 0F 06.
    - a page holds about 42 entries of short words (461 bytes were used of
      508).  A new page is started when the next entry does not fit.

Rules seen in the experiments:
    - A leading soft hyphen is ignored; two in a row are one; a soft hyphen at
      the end is kept as a bit after the last letter (it has no effect).
      No error is given when a dictionary is made.
    - A word that is already in the dictionary keeps its hyphenation when a
      document that has it again is added (zor-ba-flux stayed and zor-baf-lux
      was not taken).
    - Digits and an apostrophe are part of a word (num-ber1-23, zorbaflux's).
    - Long words are cut: a word of 28 letters with four hyphens was stored
      whole, a word of 42 letters was stored as its first 28 letters; one of 43
      letters as 32; one of 32 letters as 31.  The reason is not found.
    - Convert Hyphenation Dictionary to Document writes one block with the
      words in lower case and a soft hyphen (1F) where a word may be broken,
      one word to a line (1D), with the same header record MD "Listing of
      dictionary HQ.HYC, DATE".  The byte of a soft hyphen in a document is
      1F (seen after typing Ctrl-Hyphen in the editor).


4. The main dictionaries on the disks  [V]  NOT DECODED, NOT NEEDED
====================================================================
The disks hold the main dictionaries of the language "American English":

    spell/american.spl         174915 bytes  the spelling dictionary
    thesaurus/american.syn     327378 bytes  the thesaurus
    print_2/hyamaa05.hyp         7090 bytes  automatic hyphenation: a table
    print_2/hyamaa05.exc        46202 bytes  hyphenation exceptions

What was learned from a look at the bytes, and no more than that:

    - hyamaa05.exc begins with the text "VERSION 005" and NUL, then 16-bit
      little endian fields (03 00, 1E 00, 00 01, 24 00, 06 00, ...), the
      alphabet "'-.abcdefghijklmnopqrstuvwxyz" (29 characters), a table of
      code lengths (0F 08 17 08 0C 11 ...) and from 0x100 packed data that
      is not text.  It is a compressed list of the words that the automatic
      rules get wrong, with their break places, built to be read by the
      original's program only.
    - hyamaa05.hyp begins with 16-bit fields, a table that folds the accented
      letters (CP437 81 82 84 86 89 8A 8B 94 E1) to the letters ueaaeeios,
      and then a table of 16-bit words (most of them 0065): the rules of the
      automatic hyphenation, a table of letter patterns.
    - american.spl is a compressed word list; the manual calls its words
      "all derived forms except the possessive" (90,000 words of Webster).

These files were NOT decoded.  Strata does not need them: it has no
dictionary of its own.  The words of the spell check come from aspell or
hunspell and the hyphenation of the print comes from the patterns of TeX
(English, whatever the language of the document: manual, Print chapter).
To copy the original's exceptions into Strata one would have to decode the
.exc file (days of work with an uncertain result), and there is no use for
it: the patterns of TeX and the personal list of section 6 cover it.


5. What Strata has now
======================
Spelling  (done, see SPELL.txt and the manual, Maintain Dictionaries):
    - Maintain Dictionaries of the Document Manager shows, adds, deletes,
      imports (F3) and exports (F4) the personal word list; the spell check
      reads the list at every start; Define adds to it.  The list is a plain
      text file, one word to a line, with the same capital rule as the
      original's .SPC file, so the two are the same kind of file.
    - Import of a .SPC file of the original: works for words of ASCII.  Its
      last byte, 1A (Ctrl-Z), was counted as a line that is not a word.  It
      is now the end of the list (src/dictlist.c, tests/dicttest.c: Ctrl-Z
      ends the list and is not counted).  A word with an accented letter in
      the code page of the old program (a byte above 7F) is not UTF-8 and is
      probably refused [?]: see question 4.
    - Not done and not needed: the second custom dictionary, and the three
      conversions (the list is edited in the panel; the conversions of the
      original exist because its dictionary was a binary file).
Hyphenation  (nothing of the original is there):
    - The Printer breaks words with the patterns of TeX.  There is no way to
      say where a word may or may not be broken, no soft hyphen, no choice
      of Automatic, Custom Dictionary, Soft Hyphens or None, and none of the
      Hyphenation Settings (consecutive lines, letters before and after).
    - Ctrl-Hyphen is "no" in KEYSTROKES.txt: the key does nothing.  The soft
      hyphen of the original is the byte 1F in the text of a block (shown
      by the Editor as the triangle that points down, CP437 1F, which is
      also the key's own code in a terminal: Ctrl-Minus sends 1F).  A file
      that has such a byte today probably shows the triangle and prints it
      as a character [?]: not tried.


6. Recommendation
=================
Release 1 needs the way to say where a word may be broken, because every
technical document has words that TeX gets wrong.  The smallest set that
does it, in this order:

  R1  Soft hyphen (size M).  Ctrl-Hyphen puts the byte 1F in the text; the
      screen draws it as the triangle; the Printer writes \- at it (TeX then
      breaks the word only where the author said: a word with a soft hyphen
      is not otherwise hyphenated, as in the original); Search and Replace
      find it (Soft hyphen in the Find list, like the nonbreaking space);
      Export drops it; the spell check reads a word through it; Count does
      not count it.  Risks: the byte must not be taken for a text indent or
      a record end by the readers; the line layout must treat it as a
      zero width cell that becomes a hyphen only at a break (the screen never
      breaks lines at it, so the triangle is drawn as a character, as the
      original did).
  R2  A personal hyphenation list (size M).  A second list in Maintain
      Dictionaries (the panel gets a choice of the two lists: Spelling,
      Hyphenation), the file hyphenation in the settings folder (one word to
      a line with a hyphen where it may be broken: pro-ject; a word with no
      hyphen is never broken, as in the original), the same keys and checks as
      the spelling list (letters, digits, apostrophes; at most 65 characters).
      The Printer writes the list as \hyphenation{pro-ject ...} into the
      preamble (the words are lower case; TeX also matches a capitalised
      word).  Import of a .HYC file of the original by F3 (the layout is
      section 3: a small reader, tools/hyc.py is the model) and export to a
      text file.  Risks: a very long list slows the first LaTeX pass a little
      (100,000 entries would be too many: the list is for the words of one
      author).
  R3  Hyphenation Settings in the print panels (size S to M, after R1 and
      R2): Hyphenation (Automatic, Custom Dictionary, Soft Hyphens, None) as
      \lefthyphenmin and \righthyphenmin (Minimum Characters Before and After
      Hyphen), \hyphenpenalty at 10000 for None, and a limit of consecutive
      hyphenated lines with \doublehyphendemerits (TeX has no hard limit, so
      Maximum Consecutive Hyphenated Lines is an approximation: say so in the
      manual).  The space widths of the panel have no use with TeX.  Later.
  R4  A CP437 conversion in the import of a spelling list (size S), for words
      with accents from the original's lists (question 4).

NOT recommended: decoding american.spl, hyamaa05.hyp or hyamaa05.exc; an own
spelling checker; the three conversions of spelling dictionaries to and from
documents; a second custom spelling dictionary; the .SPC as the format of the
list (the plain text file is the same thing with a better name).

The sizes are in the work of one job: S a few hours, M a day.

8. How the research was done (for repeating it)
===============================================
The install disks of the original are in the folder lotus-manuscript/ of the
project (driver_1 has ms.exe; the other disks have the programs and the
spelling files).  A scratch DOS drive was made by copying all of them into
C:\MS and the samples into C:\MS\MSDATA.  DOSBox-X ran on a private Xvfb with
all its output in a file; a small script typed the keys with xdotool; screens
were read from xwd.  The first start of the Document Manager asks for Setup
(Ins accepts it and writes MS.VAL).  The documents were made by a script (a
document of the format of DOC-FORMAT.txt with the words as blocks and the soft
hyphens as the byte 1F) and the dictionaries read back with tools/hyc.py.  The
menu of Maintain Dictionaries remembers its last entry (Home goes to the first
one); files made outside DOSBox-X after it started are not seen until it is
restarted.

The test words and the answers of the original are in sections 2 and 3: a
document with the words zorbaflux, quimbleton, Xanthippe, BLORF, it's and
aa-bb became a .SPC file of 91 bytes; the words backhoe, bulldozer, project,
quimbleton, zorbaflux and zorbaflux's with soft hyphens became a .HYC file of
1024 bytes whose second page starts with backhoe and ends with the trailer
0B 0D 0B 0E 0D 0F 06; 300 words of two to four syllables became 4096 bytes
with the index keys defe, gras, lopo, netu, post, sami and tupl and the number
0000012C at the end of page 0.
