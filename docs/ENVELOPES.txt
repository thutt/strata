ENVELOPE SIZES AND ADDRESS POSITIONS: THE STUDY BEHIND THE ENVELOPE TEMPLATES
=============================================================================

Written 2026-11-18 for the owner's request: look up the envelope sizes of the
countries of Europe, Asia, North America and South America and compare them
with the paper size and the page layout that the templates use; and make a
set of country or region templates for the sizes and positions that can be
verified.  The templates are made by
tests/mktemplates.py (the committed copies are in templates/envelopes,
docs/TEMPLATES.txt); the positions
used in this document were MEASURED on a PDF printed from each template (the
boxes of ink, in millimetres from the top left corner of the page).

How to read this document: every number says where it comes from.  A number
that two good sources contradict, or that no source gave, is listed under
"Not verified" and no template was changed because of it.  The web was
searched in standard mode; the texts of several standards are sold (DIN 676,
DIN 680, DIN 5008, NF Z 10-011, SN 010 130, GB/T 1416, JIS S 5502) and were
not read: the figures come from postal operators and secondary sources.


1.  WHAT WAS DONE
-----------------

  - Sizes and address rules were looked up for the countries of the request
    (Section 2 and 3).
  - Every envelope template and both letter templates were printed and
    measured, and compared with the numbers found (Section 4).
  - The German sender line was 100.6 mm long, the address field of DIN 5008
    is 85 mm wide: fixed, it is 81.0 mm now.
  - The set of envelope templates grew from 5 to 14 (Section 7), and on
    2026-11-18 to 16 (Section 9): North America 3, United Kingdom 3, Germany
    1, France 1, continental Europe 3, Latin America 1, Australia 2, Japan
    2.  The German template is now called
    envelope-de-din5008-dl.  Every size of a template is checked in the
    tests against the size of the source (not against the generator), and the
    place of the address against the rules that were found.
  - What was left out, and why, is in Section 7.  What is not verified is in
    Section 6.

3.  WHERE THE ADDRESS GOES
--------------------------

  Germany (DIN 676 and DIN 5008).
    - The address field is 85 mm wide and 45 mm high and starts 20 mm from
      the left edge of the sheet (Macwelt, "Know-how Normsatz", quoting DIN
      676; Briefmarkenmesse Essen; ausbildung.de).
    - The distance from the top edge of the sheet is 45 mm in Macwelt and
      Briefmarkenmesse Essen and 50 mm in monsterdealz.de and other guides
      (for form B; form A starts higher, at 32 mm in monsterdealz).  The
      sources contradict each other; 50 mm may be the value of the edition
      of 2020.  NOT VERIFIED.
    - The sender (the return address line) may stand in the top 5 mm of the
      field, in small type (Macwelt).  The address must stand exactly in the
      window and at least 3 mm from its edge (Macwelt).
    - The window of a DL or C4 envelope is 90 mm wide and 45 mm high
      (Briefmarkenmesse Essen).  Its distance from the left and bottom edges
      was not found; a postal rule keeps the lowest 15 mm of the envelope
      free (one source): NOT VERIFIED.
    - Macwelt gives the DL envelope as 188 x 218 mm: that is not the ISO
      size (220 x 110) and is taken as an error of that article.

  United Kingdom (Royal Mail, Guide for Clear Addressing, 2026-04-21, read in
  full).
    - Delivery address block: at least 15 mm from the top, left and right
      edges and at least 18 mm from the bottom edge; landscape; the last line
      of the geographic address more than 50 mm from the top edge; no part
      more than 125 mm above the bottom edge; at most 64 characters a line;
      one block only.
    - The indicia area is the top right corner: 40 mm high and 75 mm from the
      right edge (a little more for the larger designs); the address block
      must stay out of it.
    - Return address on the front, top left corner, no lower than 28 mm from
      the top edge, no closer than 12 mm to the delivery address; on the back
      within 40 mm of the top.
    - Windows: at least 15 mm from the left, right and top and at least 18 mm
      from the bottom edge; at most 50 percent of the surface; rectangular
      with rounded corners on the front.

  France (La Poste, NF Z 10-011, SP 8855).
    - At most 6 lines of 38 characters; the last line in capitals; no
      punctuation from line 4 on (La Poste, SP 8855 volume 2, version 1.14).
    - The press mailing specification (SP 8855 volume 4, version 1.14, for
      publications) puts the address zone at least 20 mm from the right edge
      and from the bottom edge, the top of its first line at most 120 mm from
      the bottom edge, and its left edge at least half the width from the
      left edge.  It is a rule for publications in bulk, not for a business
      letter, and is NOT VERIFIED for ordinary letters.
    - The positions in NF Z 10-011 itself were not read (it is sold).

  United States (Domestic Mail Manual 202, archived edition of 2016-01-17).
    - Address inside the optical character reader area: at least 1/2 inch
      (12.7 mm) from the left and right edges, the area reaching 2 3/4
      inches (69.9 mm) up from the bottom edge, and not lower than 5/8 inch
      (15.9 mm) from the bottom edge.  The current edition was not read.

  Canada (Canada Post, Machineable Mail, current page).
    - The address window is placed in the address zone, 100 mm from the
      right side of the item; 7 mm to 10 mm of clear space around the address
      block; 7 mm between windows.  The wording leaves open whether the
      block lies within 100 mm of the right edge or begins 100 mm from it:
      NOT VERIFIED.

  Brazil (Correios, CEPNet guide, 2021): the address is read by machine
    (CEP, postal code, in the address block); no distance from the edges was
    read.  NOT VERIFIED.

  Not found: Mexico, Argentina, Chile, Colombia, Peru, Australia, Russia,
  Switzerland, Japan (horizontal), China, India, South Korea, Singapore
  (window and address zone distances).

5.  WHAT WAS CHANGED
--------------------

  tests/mktemplates.py
    - The sender line of envelope-de-din5008-dl: "Strata GmbH - Hauptstr. 1 -
      12345 Musterstadt" (81.0 mm printed; it was 100.6 mm, 14.9 mm past the
      DIN field).
    - The German template is renamed from envelope-de-dl to
      envelope-de-din5008-dl.
    - Nine envelopes are added: envelope-us-ca-9, envelope-us-ca-monarch,
      envelope-uk-c6, envelope-eu-dl, envelope-eu-c5, envelope-eu-c65,
      envelope-latam-dl, envelope-au-dl, envelope-au-c5.  Each has a
      description (at most 250 characters) with the country or region, the
      size and what the paper is.  The sizes that the Paper Size list does
      not have (No. 9, C6, C6/5) use the paper Custom with their width and
      height.
  templates/: the 14 envelopes and the 2 letters, made again with the
    generator; the old envelope-de-dl.doc is removed.
  tests/templates.py
    - SOURCES: the size of every envelope in millimetres from the sources, the
      size text that the description must name and the rule set; the printed
      page must have the size of the source within 0.6 mm (a wrong size in
      the generator fails).
    - The recipient must meet the rule set of its template: Royal Mail
      margins, the reading area of the Postal Service, the DIN field; the
      sender no lower than 28 mm and not closer than 12 mm to the recipient.
    - The folder of templates holds the generator's templates and no other
      (a renamed template leaves no old file); 12 to 20 envelopes.
  tests/installtest.py: the installed folder holds exactly the 16 templates
    of the generator, with mode 644 and in the manifest; the New list of the
    installed Editor shows all 16 names and the description of the chosen
    one.
  manual/src/file3.src: the table of the shipped templates, the table of the
    places of the address and a paragraph that says to take the template of
    the same size where a country has none of its own.
  docs/FILE-MENU.txt names the set.

  Mutations (each run: change the generator, make the templates again, run
  python3 -I -B tests/templates.py, restore):
    M1  us-ca-9 narrower by 5 mm              -> fails (the size)
    M2  uk-c6 recipient 18 mm higher          -> fails (the Royal Mail check)
    M3  us-ca-monarch recipient 34 mm lower   -> fails (the Postal Service)
    M4  the size left out of the description of eu-c65 -> fails
    M5  the C5 envelopes 5 mm lower           -> fails (the size)


6.  NOT VERIFIED  (no template was changed for these, and none was made)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

  1.  (DECIDED 2026-11-18, Section 9.1: 45 mm; the 50 mm is the window.)
      DIN 5008 form B: the top of the address field at 45 mm or at 50 mm.
  2.  DIN 680: the window of the DL envelope, its distance from the left and
      the bottom edge (only its size, 90 x 45 mm, was found).
  3.  (DECIDED 2026-11-18, Section 9.2.)  The positions of NF Z 10-011 for La
      Poste (letters).  The specification of La Poste gives half the width
      for the left edge of the address; the template used 99 mm and uses
      113 mm now.
  4.  Canada Post: whether the address block lies within 100 mm of the right
      edge.  The current DMM of the United States (the 2016 edition was
      read).
  5.  GB/T 1416-2021 (China): No. 5 (220 x 110) is certain through another
      source; the other numbers of the standard were not read.  No Chinese
      template (the position of the postcode boxes was not found).
  6.  JIS S 5502 itself: the sizes are from the table of Japan Post.  The
      Japanese templates of Section 9.4 follow the text of Japan Post and the
      windows of the japanlco files of KOMA-Script; the figures of the
      standard were not read (sold).
  7.  SN 010 130 (Switzerland): sizes from shop listings only.
  8.  South Korea and Singapore: nothing found.  India: two pages of India
      Post disagree on the sizes; no Indian template.
  9.  Window position in US window envelopes (No. 10 with a window), and in
      the window envelopes of any country but Germany (and there only the
      width).
  10. Mexico, Argentina, Chile, Colombia, Peru: no postal source for the
      envelope sizes and positions was found; the sizes in Latin America come
      from retail sources (the "sobre americano" 110 x 220 mm; Chile also
      sells 105 x 228 and 107 x 228) and, for Brazil, from the Correios guide
      (minimum 140 x 90 mm).  Brazil: the figure of the minimum is
      contradicted by two blogs (160 x 110 mm).
  11. The position of the address in envelope-eu-*, envelope-latam-dl and
      envelope-uk-c6 and of the sender in all of them is a layout that meets
      the rules of Royal Mail (read in full) and the address field of DIN
      5008; it is NOT the national rule of Italy, Spain, the Netherlands,
      Sweden, Poland, Switzerland, Russia or Latin America (none was found;
      Section 9.3).  The Australian templates meet the rule of Australia Post
      (read on 2026-11-18, Section 9.3).
  12. Royal Mail guide: window figures are from the guide of 2026; an older
      document says the same (15 mm and 18 mm).


7.  THE SHIPPED SET AND WHAT WAS LEFT OUT
-----------------------------------------

  Shipped (16 envelopes and 2 letters, a list that can be scanned in New;
  grouped by region here; Japan was added on 2026-11-18):

    North America   envelope-us-ca-10, envelope-us-ca-9,
                    envelope-us-ca-monarch
    Latin America   envelope-latam-dl
    United Kingdom  envelope-uk-c5, envelope-uk-c6, envelope-uk-dl
    Germany         envelope-de-din5008-dl
    France          envelope-fr-dl
    Europe          envelope-eu-c5, envelope-eu-c65, envelope-eu-dl
    Australia       envelope-au-c5, envelope-au-dl
    Japan           envelope-jp-yougata-4, envelope-jp-yougata-2
    Letters         letter-a4, letter-us

  Left out, and why:
    - Japan, nagagata and kakugata (the long and the square envelopes): the
      common ones are addressed in vertical writing, which the Editor and the
      Printer do not have.  The Western-style envelopes (yougata) addressed
      horizontally are shipped (Section 9.4).
    - China (No. 5 to 7): the sizes are known only for No. 5 (the DL size);
      the position of the postcode boxes was not found.  China's No. 5 is
      served by envelope-eu-dl in size.
    - India, South Korea, Singapore, New Zealand: no sizes or positions that
      two sources agree on (India), or no source (the others).  New Zealand
      uses the ISO sizes; the Australian templates have the same sizes.
    - Mexico, Argentina, Chile, Colombia, Peru: no source for the positions;
      envelope-latam-dl has the common size, the sobre americano.
    - A C5 template for Latin America and Canada: no source says they use it
      (in Canada a C5 is above the standard letter size).
    - C4 (324 x 229 mm, A4 flat) and a German C5 or C6/5 window envelope:
      the position of the window (DIN 680) and the rules for large letters
      were not found.
    - Further American sizes (No. 6 3/4, No. 11, A2 to A10): not business
      envelopes, or the position rules of the Postal Service for them were not
      read; they can be made with the Paper Size list and the Custom size.

  Positions that need the owner's word or a source: (a) letter-a4: the
  recipient block down by 5 mm if the 50 mm of DIN 5008 is confirmed; (b)
  envelope-fr-dl: the recipient 110 mm from the left if the press rule is
  meant to apply.

9.  DECISIONS OF 2026-11-18  (the owner: figure the open questions out;
    horizontal Japanese envelopes: yes)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

9.1  DIN 5008 form B: the address field begins 45 mm from the top of the
     letter.  The 50 mm of other guides is the top of the WINDOW on the
     ENVELOPE.
  - The Bavarian regulation BayAGO Anlage 2 (a copy of the layout of DIN 676)
    says that form B has a letterhead field of 45 mm and form A one of 27 mm
    (the address field lies below it: 45 mm and 27 mm from the top).  Macwelt
    and Briefmarkenmesse Essen say 45 mm.  The guides that say 50 mm
    (monsterdealz.de, a handout of Wirtschaftsdeutsch) give the window of the
    envelope: the DL window of DIN 680 is 90 by 45 mm, 20 mm from the left and
    15 mm from the bottom edge (data sheets of printers of window envelopes:
    flyerdevil.de, wir-machen-druck.ch); an envelope is 110 mm high, so the
    window begins 110 - 15 - 45 = 50 mm from the top edge of the envelope.  A
    letter folded in three by DIN 5008 (fold marks at 105 and 210 mm) is 105
    mm high, so it rests 5 mm lower in the envelope than its top edge and the
    field of 45 mm (45 + 5 = 50 mm) is in the window.  Both numbers are
    right; they are two different edges.
  - The address zone of the field begins 17.7 mm below its top (five lines
    of 8 point above it; the first is the return line): 62.7 mm from the top
    of the letter.  Two guides give another count of the lines of the zone
    (three lines in an older edition); the 2020 guide says five.  The text of
    DIN 5008 was not read (sold).
  - Changed: the recipient of letter-a4 moved from 50.3 mm to 62.7 mm from the
    top of the sheet (the date and the lines after it move with it).  The
    German envelope (the letter in the middle of the envelope: 5 mm of play
    across): text 25 mm from the left edge, the return line 54 mm and the
    recipient 67.7 mm from the top, all of it 3 mm or more inside the window.
  - Tests (tests/templates.py): the window of DIN 680 and the folded letter
    are computed from the numbers of the sources; the recipient of letter-a4
    is in the address zone and, for every place of the folded letter in the
    envelope (10 mm of play across, 5 mm up and down), 3 mm or more inside the
    window.

9.2  La Poste: the address of an envelope goes in the right half.
  - The only document of La Poste with figures that was read is the
    specification of the pli (SP 8855 volume 4, version 1.13 and 1.14):
    the address zone is 20 mm or more from the right edge (D3) and from the
    bottom edge (D4), the top of its first line 120 mm or less above the
    bottom edge (D5), its left edge half the width or more from the left edge
    (D6), and the sender block is at the top left (RG 4-7).  It is written for
    items in bulk (courier industriel and the press), and NF Z 10-011 is sold.
  - Decision: apply it to the template for ordinary letters too.  It is the one
    rule of La Poste with numbers, it costs nothing (an address in the right
    half is where every reading machine looks) and it also meets any narrower
    rule.  envelope-fr-dl: the recipient moved from 99 mm to 113 mm from the
    left edge; the description says that La Poste asks for it.
  - Test: the address is half the width or more from the left edge, 20 mm or
    more from the right and the bottom edge, its first line 120 mm or less
    above the bottom edge; the sender is at the top left.

9.3  The templates of continental Europe, Latin America and Australia.
  - Australia (Australia Post, PreSort Letters guide, section 2.5.1 for the
    articles up to C5, read in full on 2026-11-18): the barcode and delivery
    address are 15 mm or more from the bottom edge, 100 mm or less up from
    it, 10 mm or more from the left and the right edge and out of the postage
    zone (90 mm wide from the top right corner, 40 mm down; the return
    address must not infringe it).  envelope-au-dl and envelope-au-c5 meet it
    as they are; the tests now check exactly this rule set and the
    descriptions say so.
  - Continental Europe (eu-dl, eu-c5, eu-c65) and Latin America (latam-dl): no
    national rule was found.  KEPT, none pulled: the sizes are certain, the
    layout meets the margins of Royal Mail, and the description now says in
    plain words that the layout keeps the margins of Royal Mail and that no
    national rule was found, so it does not mislead.  The pull of a template
    would remove the only template of that size.
  - envelope-uk-c6: unchanged, it meets Royal Mail.

9.3a Changes that touch several templates: the descriptions of eu-dl, eu-c5,
     eu-c65 and latam-dl are 197 to 201 characters (the limit is 250); the
     tests check the limit.

9.4  Horizontal Japanese envelopes (yougata).
  - Sizes (Japan Post, JIS S 5502:2010 table, read on 2026-11-18): Yougata 2
    162 x 114 mm, Yougata 4 235 x 105 mm (the others of the table: Yougata 1
    176 x 120, Yougata 6 190 x 98; nagagata and kakugata sizes as in Section
    2).  Paper (japanlco files of KOMA-Script): Yougata 2 takes A5 folded in
    half or A4 folded in four, Yougata 4 takes A4 folded in three.
  - Position: the left window of the standard, from the lco files of
    KOMA-Script for Chou and You 3 and 4 (window 90 x 45 mm, 22 or 25 mm from
    the left edge, 12 mm from the top); Japan Post's rules for writing
    horizontally (the page of the envelope manners of Japan Post): the postal
    code at the top left, the address below it starting about two characters
    from the left edge, the sender on the BACK in the lower third.  So the
    templates have no sender block.
  - envelope-jp-yougata-4: size 235 x 105 mm; the recipient (postal code
    first) 28 mm from the left and 15 mm from the top, inside the window and
    3 mm from its edges; test: the ink is 3 mm or more inside the window.
  - envelope-jp-yougata-2: size 162 x 114 mm; the address starts 10 mm (about
    two characters) from the left edge and 15 mm from the top.  The standard
    gives no window for this size and Japan Post's figures are in images that
    were not available as text: the vertical position (15 mm) is by analogy
    with the Yougata 4 and is NOT VERIFIED.
  - Left out: Nagagata 3 and 4 and the Kakugata (they are addressed
    vertically; a horizontal address on them is not in any rule that was
    found), Yougata 1 and 6 (no rule), and the sender on the back (the
    Editor prints one side of an envelope).  The address text of the templates
    is in ASCII (a template description and sample text are ASCII); replace it
    by your own.
  - Not verified: the figures of JIS S 5502 and of the Japan Post pages that
    are images (the horizontal address zones, 'yoko-gaki'); the sizes come
    from the text table of Japan Post and the windows from KOMA-Script.

10.  THE SAMPLE ENVELOPES  (the owner, 2026-11-19, worklist W54: "sample
     envelope documents with a variety of envelope styles")

10.1  What they are
  There is one sample for each of the 16 envelope templates, in
  samples/envelopes/ of the source tree (sample-envelope-NAME.doc, NAME the
  name of the template).  A sample is the document that New makes from its
  template, with the address of a person or a company that does not exist,
  written as the country of the envelope writes it: the attention line and
  ZIP+4 in the United States, the postal code of Canada, capitals in France,
  the province after the town in Italy, the colony in Mexico, the postal
  code first and "sama" in Japan.  The paper, the named formats and the
  places of the blocks are those of the template, so a sample prints where
  its template does.  A sample has a description of its own ("Sample: ..."),
  and it is a document, not a template: the list of New does not show it.

10.2  Where they are  (decision)
  The folder is samples/envelopes/.  manual/samples belongs to the manual
  (the documents of its pictures and of the Tutorial) and tests/samples to
  the tests (they are installed as share/strata/samples/*): a sample that a
  user is meant to open has a folder of its own.  The install puts them in
  share/strata/samples/envelopes/ (the manifest and the list of files of the
  Getting Started document name them), and a build folder has a link of that
  name to the folder of the source tree, as it has for the templates
  (Makefile, templates-link), so that a program run in place finds them with
  no variable.

10.3  How they are made and checked
  tests/mksamples.py makes them, from the template generator
  (tests/mktemplates.py envelope(), which takes the description and the lines
  of the sender and of the recipient as arguments); there is no date in them
  and two runs make the same bytes.  tests/envsamples.py (in the smoke) checks
  that the folder is what the generator makes, that every sample opens in the
  Editor, prints to one page of the size of its envelope with the address in
  the zone of its style (tests/envzones.py: the numbers of the sources of
  Section 8 and 9, not those of the generator), that none names a product of
  another maker, and that the manual lists every sample and has its pictures.
  tests/installtest.py checks the installed copies.  The test found one fault
  in the first version of a sample: the return line of the German sample ran
  0.4 mm out of the window of DIN 680; it is shorter now.

11. THE PAPER OF AN ENVELOPE IN THE PAGE PANEL, AND THE PREVIEW
---------------------------------------------------------------
(2026-11-19, two owner reports: the paper of the US number 9 envelope was
shown as a letter size, and the Preview of an envelope was not shown the
right way up.)

The paper.  The Paper Size field of Global, Page is a choice in a list of
named papers (src/globalmenu.c, papers[]).  The list stays as it is: its
order is the number that the DP record stores first, so inserting an entry
would change every saved document.  The number 9 envelope, C6, C65 and the
two Japanese sizes are not in the list; they are stored as the last entry,
Custom, with their real width and length (DP16,8875,3875 for the number 9).
The text of the Custom entry was the fixed words Custom (8.5 in x 11 in), so
every one of them looked like a letter sized paper.  Now:
  - the field takes the paper of the stored size, either way round (an
    envelope is stored long side first, the list gives the short side
    first); a stored size that no named paper has is Custom, never the paper
    of the stored index; a record with an index and no size has the paper of
    the index;
  - the Custom entry names the real size: the shorter side first, in
    millimetres when both sides are whole millimetres (Custom (114 x 162
    mm)), else in inches as eighths or thousandths (Custom (3 7/8 x 8 7/8
    in));
  - choosing Custom keeps the size of the document; for a document with a
    named paper it is 8.5 by 11 inches, as before;
  - accepting the panel with no change writes nothing (the file is the same
    bytes).
Table of the templates (before: the text of the field; after):
  envelope-us-ca-9       Custom (8.5 in x 11 in)  ->  Custom (3 7/8 x 8 7/8 in)
  envelope-uk-c6         Custom (8.5 in x 11 in)  ->  Custom (114 x 162 mm)
  envelope-eu-c65        Custom (8.5 in x 11 in)  ->  Custom (114 x 229 mm)
  envelope-jp-yougata-4  Custom (8.5 in x 11 in)  ->  Custom (105 x 235 mm)
  envelope-jp-yougata-2  Custom (8.5 in x 11 in)  ->  Custom (114 x 162 mm)
The other eleven envelope templates and the two letters show the paper that
they showed before (DL, C5, Business, Monarch, A4, Letter); tests/
papernames.py checks all 18 templates and the 16 sample envelopes.

The Preview.  The page of the Preview is the page of the print: the Printer
writes the DVI with a papersize special, and a page that an Orientation
marker turns carries the special pdf: put @thispage <</Rotate 90>>, which
the Viewer honours (tools/dviview/src/special.c).  The Preview of every
template and sample envelope was checked against the PDF of the same
document (the same width and length, upright text).  Two faults were found
and fixed:
  - an envelope chosen in the paper list was stored with the short side as
    the width (DP13,4125,9500), so its page was tall and narrow; the four
    envelope entries are now stored long side first, as the templates store
    them;
  - the part of a document that Preview Block, Preview Section and the print
    of a block or a section make starts after an Orientation (landscape)
    marker that is not in the part, so it was drawn upright; the part now
    carries the header record PO2 (src/fileops.c part_items, written by
    doc_serialize, read by the Printer), and a whole document has no PO
    record.
The print queue: lp gets the number of copies, the sides, the collation and
the page range, and no orientation or media option; the PDF carries the page
size and the rotation, which the print system uses.

12. THE OWNER'S PRINT OF AN ENVELOPE: ORIENTATION, RETURN ADDRESS, TYPE SIZE
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
(2026-11-19.  A real print: with the page set to an envelope the envelope
printed in portrait, the narrow edge entering the printer, and the return
address ran along the short edge; with US Office paper the envelope fed
correctly in the manual feed and printed the right way, but the return

12.1 WHAT THE FIRST FIX MISSED, AND WHAT THE SECOND ONE DOES
------------------------------------------------------------
(2026-11-20.  The owner printed again: a United States number 9 envelope
still had small type and the return address far from the corner.)

What was missed.  The first fix changed the templates and the orientation of
the entries of the list.  It did not change what happens to a document whose
paper is CHOSEN in the Paper Size list:
  - The number 9 envelope (3 7/8 x 8 7/8 inches) was not in the list at all.
    It was Custom, entry 16, with the size stored.  The rule "no PS record and
    an envelope paper has 14 points" tested the entries 12 to 15 of the list
    by their index, so Custom, and any size stored under another index, was
    set in 11 points (a measured print: 10 point face, first word 96.7 points
    from the left, 105 points from the top: 1.34 and 1.46 inches).
  - Choosing an envelope changed only the paper.  The document kept its page
    margins of one inch and its type size, so the text stood one inch inside
    the corner.  Only a template has zero margins and the 14 points.

What is done now.
  - One table of envelope sizes (doc_envelope_points in src/msdoc.c), the
    sizes of the paper list and of the templates: Monarch, number 10,
    number 9, DL, C5, C6/5, C6 and Yougata 2 (the same size), Yougata 4.  The
    Printer takes 14 points for ANY page of one of these sizes, in either
    orientation and whatever index or Custom size the record DP names, when
    the document has no record PS.  Every size has 14 points; a template that
    needs another size has its own record (German DL 11, European C5 12).
  - The paper list has the number 9 envelope, the C6/5, the C6 (the Japanese
    Yougata 2 is the same size) and the Yougata 4.  Custom is still stored as
    entry 16 as before; a stored size is found in the list by its size.
  - Choosing an envelope in the Paper Size list sets, once, at the choice, the
    four page margins to half an inch (Left, Right, Top and Bottom Margin of
    the same panel) and the record PS, so that the sender stands half an inch
    from the left edge and a little more from the top (measured: 36 points
    from the left, 40 points from the top, in 14 point type).  A margin that
    the same visit of the panel changed keeps the value that was typed; a
    record PS that the document has stays; choosing another paper later
    leaves the margins as they are.  The change is one step of Undo.  A
    template made with the Template field is not changed by this: it already
    has its zero margins and its own indents (sender at 0.44 inch from the
    left and 0.52 inch from the top, 14 points).
  - For a page of an envelope size the Printer gives lp the option
    -o print-scaling=none.  Before, lp got no scaling option, so CUPS applied
    its own default (print-scaling auto: fit the page to the printable area),
    which can shrink the type and move the corner, and chose its own media for
    an odd page size.  A letter or an A4 still has the default of the system.
    No media option is sent: a media name that the printer does not know can
    make it hold the job.

NOT TESTED ON PAPER.  The PDF was measured and the arguments of lp were
tested, no envelope was printed.  What to look at first if an envelope
still prints wrong:
  1. Open the PDF that the Printer made (the Output File of the Print panel,
     or the same print with the printer set to File only).  If the PDF is
     right (page 9 by 3 7/8 inches, 14 point type, the sender near the
     corner) the difference is in the print system, not in Strata.
  2. The print system then decides the media and the scaling: set the media
     of the printer for envelopes (in the printer's own settings: the envelope
     size and the manual feed as the paper source) and check that the job
     shows scaling "none" (lpoptions or the CUPS web page of the job).  A
     print system may also turn the page by its own rule (the narrow edge
     enters the printer); the PDF page is 9 inches across, as the templates
     store it.

12.2 THE SECOND FIX MISSED A BUSINESS ENVELOPE OF 4.25 INCHES
-------------------------------------------------------------
(2026-11-20.  The owner's envelopes are number 10 envelopes.  The number 10
still printed in small type: the second fix had been tested with the exact
size of the list, 9.5 x 4.125 inches.)

What was missed.  doc_envelope_points compared the stored page size with the
table of sizes to within 0.002 inch.  A Custom page of 9.5 x 4.25 inches, a
business envelope that measures a quarter inch more than the list says, was
not an envelope: 10 point type (measured: LMRoman10), the same for 9.5 x 4.5
and for 9.25 x 4.0.  The owner's own envelope, 240 x 104 mm (9.449 x 4.094
inches), is within 0.002 and 0.001 inch of the number 10 of the table and was
always recognised.

What the rule is now (one function, doc_envelope_points, for the Printer and
for the Editor):
  - A page is an envelope when each side is within 0.30 inch of a size of
    the table, in either orientation: 9.5 x 4.25, 9.5 x 4.5, 9.25 x 4.0, a
    C5 of 9.2 x 6.5 and a C6 of 6.5 x 4.5 are envelopes.
  - Or, whatever the table says, when it has the proportions of one: the
    long side at least twice the short side, the short side between 2.5 and
    5 inches, the long side at most 11.5 inches (9.5 x 4.75 and 10 x 4.5 are
    envelopes this way).
  - Letter, A4, a 6 x 9 inch page, a half letter (5.5 x 8.5), an index card
    (4 x 6), a 5 x 5 square and a 6.5 x 3.6 inch page are not envelopes: they
    keep their size of type and their margins.
  What the rule gives for 240 x 104 mm (9449 x 4094 thousandths of an inch):
  an envelope by the table (9500 x 4125, 51 and 31 thousandths away) and by
  the proportions (2.31), in either orientation: 14 points.
An envelope recognised only by its proportions has 14 points; one recognised
by the table has the size of its entry.

Not changed.  The Paper Size panel has no field to type a size: its Custom
entry shows the size that the document has.  An envelope that a document has
as a Custom size, with the margins of a page, is therefore not given the
margins of an envelope by the Editor until an envelope of the list is chosen
(which sets the size, the margins and the type size together); the Printer
gives it the type size at once.

12.3 OWNER DECISION 2026-11-20: THE RETURN ADDRESS IS 7 MM FROM THE TOP AND
LEFT EDGES (5 MM AT FIRST)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
The owner measured a print of the number 10 template with the old numbers:
the sender 11 mm from the left and 15 mm from the top of the paper.  The PDF
had 11.25 mm and 13.2 mm for the first glyph box (the left indent of 0.443
inch and the top margin of 0.461 inch), so the PDF values are what is set.
His printer, an HP LaserJet 2015PN, prints to about 4 mm from the edges, so
5 mm was allowed.  The decision: the first glyph box of the first line of the
sender (xMin and yMin of the first word in pdftotext -bbox) is 5 mm from the
left edge and 5 mm from the top edge, within 0.5 mm, for EVERY envelope that
has a sender, whatever the country.

Owner decision 2026-11-20: 7 mm (was 5 mm; the first print on the HP
LaserJet 2015dn showed 11 mm left and 15 mm top for the old half inch
margins): "Increase the left and top margins of the return address by 2 mm."
Every number below that says 5 mm is now 7 mm: xMin and yMin are 19.843
points, the left margin is 276 thousandths of an inch, and the top margins
are the old values plus 79.

How it is made.
  - The left margin is 7 mm (record PML276).  The sender has no block indent.
    The xMin of the first word is the left margin, so xMin = 19.84 points.
  - The top margin depends on the type size: the glyph box of a line begins
    below the top margin by 3.475 points at 8 point type, 3.443 at 11, 3.718
    at 12 and 4.063 at 14 (measured in the PDF with a top margin of 0 and of
    0.100 inch), so the top margin that puts yMin at 19.843 points (7 mm) is
    228, 237, 228, 230, 224, 219, 204 and 195 thousandths of an inch at 8, 9,
    10, 11, 12, 14, 17 and 20 points (the values for 5 mm were 149, 158, 149,
    151, 145, 140, 125 and 116).  The templates have 219 (14 points, the
    number 9, number 10, Monarch, DL and C6 of several countries), 230 (the
    German DL, 11 points) and 224 (the European C5, 12 points).
  - The recipient keeps its place: the Whitespace marker between the blocks is
    made so that the recipient is where the study of each country puts it; the
    indent of the recipient is counted in cells of the page width less the
    left margin and is never more than half a millimetre left of its place.
  - The Editor sets these margins when an envelope is chosen in the Paper Size
    list (the left margin 276, the top margin of the type size of the
    envelope, right and bottom half an inch).
  - The German DIN 5008 window envelope has its sender in the corner too,
    which is the owner's decision: before, a small return line stood inside
    the window.  The window now shows only the recipient.
  - The Japanese Yougata templates have no sender block (the sender goes on
    the back): their structure and positions are not changed.
Measured first glyph boxes of the sample envelopes (xMin, yMin in mm): 5.00 and

13. THE TEMPLATES THAT COME WITH STRATA HOLD NO TEXT; A SAMPLE IS AN EXAMPLE
    (2026-11-20)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

The owner asked why a template gives a sample address.  The templates that
come with Strata should not: the first ones were written with example text, a
sample company, Ms. Jane Example and a letter body, and that was a mistake.  A
template itself can hold text, such as the return address of its user; the
owner only wants no predetermined text in the templates that Strata makes.
The rule now:

  A TEMPLATE THAT COMES WITH STRATA holds the page set-up (paper, margins),
  the size of the text (the record PS), the named formats, the head of a
  letter, the places of the blocks and the blocks themselves as empty
  paragraphs.  It holds no text.  The only field in it is the date of a
  letter, a Time marker.  A new document from a template starts the cursor in
  its first block.  A template that a user writes can hold text, and a new
  document made from it holds that text.

  A SAMPLE is a finished document with example text: samples/envelopes (one
  for each envelope template) and samples/letters.  It is an ordinary
  document, not a template, and it is the only place for example text.

Both are made from one description of the layout, tests/mktemplates.py:
envelope() and letter() write empty blocks, and write the example lines of
the table ENVELOPES (or those that tests/mksamples.py and tests/mkletters.py
give) when the argument example=True or a sample asks for them.  The
argument --example of the program writes the templates with that text; the
tests print those copies to measure the places of the blocks, and check that
the shipped templates print none of the text.

The sender of an envelope is its first block, so that the cursor starts in
it.  Its place down the envelope is the top margin of the page (the distance
from the top edge less 1 mm); the recipient follows at the height of a
Whitespace marker between the two blocks, made for the number of lines that
the sender has when it is written, so that the recipient is where the address
zone is.  Before this change a Whitespace marker block came first and the
cursor started in it.
