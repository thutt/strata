The owner's report (2026-11-19): "Moving the cursor through a picture isn't
right.  It's one line: the image.  But the cursor needs many presses to get
through it, as if it's occupying a whole bunch of lines.  This is probably
related to the jump-scroll of bringing images in."  A second remark: "If you
make one key press move past the image, you will be back to jump scrolling the
entire image in."  Both must hold: one key crosses the picture, and the
screen does not leap.

1. WHAT WAS WRONG (measured before the change)
----------------------------------------------
A Picture marker is one token of the text, and the X11 window draws its image
over many rows of the screen (11 rows for the test picture of 240 by 160
pixels, many more for a tall one).  The layout gives the picture a row for
each row of the image, so the cursor walked the rows:

  - the W18 fix (2026-11-18) made Up and Down step down the rows of the
    picture one row for each key, so that the window moved one row for each
    key and did not leap (before that fix the window leapt by the whole
    height of the picture: 1, 3 and 11 rows in three keys);
  - the cost: crossing the 11 rows of the test picture took 11 keys, and
    bringing it onto the screen from the bottom took 9 (tests/xpicscroll.py
    of that time: "9 keys for 11 rows", "grows by at most 2 rows for each
    key");
  - the end of line mark of the picture's block has a row of its own under
    the image (the image fills its rows), and it was one more stop.

2. THE CHOICES AND WHY
----------------------
The picture is one line, so one key onto it and one key off it.  The window
has to move by about the height of the image when the cursor goes onto a
picture or off one, and that move must not be a leap.

  (a) Glide the window in steps (CHOSEN).  The cursor moves at once; the
      window moves to its new place in steps of at most 4 rows, 10 ms apart,
      14 steps at the most (about 150 ms), and a key that is typed meanwhile
      ends the steps at once (the key is handled, the window is drawn in
      its final place).  A frame is drawn for each step; the cursor is
      hidden while the window glides.
  (b) A picture taller than the text area (CHOSEN, with (a)).  It cannot be
      seen at once.  Page Up, Page Down, Up and Down then move the cursor
      down and up the rows of the picture a window at a time, with the
      cursor staying on the picture, until the last or first row has been
      shown; only then does the key leave the picture.  A picture that fits
      in the text area is crossed with one key.
  (c) Keep the cursor row where it is on the screen and let the text slide
      up under it.  That is what the glide does; without the glide it is the
      leap (the "leap" mutation below: the window moved 13 rows in one
      drawing).
  (d) A picture at the bottom edge is drawn clipped, not pulled in whole
      (CHOSEN): when the cursor goes onto a picture from above the window
      scrolls only as far as the first row of the image needs, so the top
      edge shows (3 rows in the test), and the next key slides the window by
      the rest, in steps.  Pulling the whole picture in at the moment of
      arrival was tried on paper and rejected: it is the leap again, only one
      key earlier.

3. THE RULES
------------
  - Down from the line above puts the cursor on the first row of the image;
    the next Down puts it on the first line below; Up is the same upwards.
    The cursor shows on the first row of the image.
  - The end of line mark under the image is not a stop of Up and Down.  Right
    and End still reach it.  Arriving at it by Up puts the cursor on the
    picture.
  - Left and Right step over the marker as over one character (unchanged).
  - Page Up and Page Down on a picture that fits the text area act as before
    (they go half a screen of lines; the picture counts as one line).
  - The glide starts only when the cursor goes onto a picture, off one, or
    down the rows of a tall picture (Editor.glide in src/editor.h, set in
    src/move.c) and the window moves by 3 rows or more.  Nothing else glides.
  - The terminal draws a picture as one row with its name in brackets (the
    terminal has no images), so a picture is already one stop there; the
    glide never happens there.
  - Displayed equations (marker 9 in a block of its own) are two rows high in
    the test and are crossed with one key each way; the same skip of the end
    of line mark applies to them.  Inline equations and keycaps are one line
    (inline equations are passed over as before).  Tables are not touched:
    a table row of several screen rows is a line of text in each column.

4. THE CODE
-----------
  src/move.c   vertical() is a wrapper of vertical_step() that sets the glide
               flag; pic_eol_row() finds the row of the end of line mark under
               a picture; arrive() puts the cursor on the picture when it
               arrives upwards at that row; picture_page() scrolls through a
               tall picture for Page Up, Page Down, Up and Down.
  src/view.c   ed_picture_rows() and ed_set_picture_row() keep the row of a
               tall picture that the cursor shows (unchanged).
  src/ui.c     glide() moves the window in steps from draw_base(); the file
               that STRATA_SCROLL_LOG names (a test aid) gets a line for each
               step and a line that starts with "abort" when a key ended the
               steps.
  src/term.h, src/termbuf.c, src/term.c, src/term_x11.c
               term_key_pending() is true only for a waiting KEY, not for an
               exposure, a pointer event or a resize.  (The first version
               used term_input_pending, which is true for any X event: the
               glide stopped after three steps and the window leapt for the
               rest.)

5. THE TESTS
------------
  tests/xpicscroll.py (X11, in xtest): one Down puts the cursor on the
  picture (its first row, the top edge of the image shows); one more Down puts
  it on the first line below; the window moved in steps of at most 6 rows
  (4, 4, 4, 1 measured), at most 14 steps; the same going up from far below;
  two and seven fast keys end in the right place; a key typed during a glide
  ends it; a picture taller than the window is scrolled through in 3 keys
  with the cursor on it and steps of at most 6 rows; a structured document.
  tests/picturecursor.py (terminal, in the smoke): Down and Up go line by line
  through a first, a middle, a doubled and a last picture, structured and not.
  This is a guard only: with a picture on one row the terminal behaved like
  this before.

  Mutations of the program, each of which makes tests/xpicscroll.py fail:
    noglide      (GLIDE_MIN 100000: the window leaps)            4 failures
    leap         (GLIDE_ROWS 400: one step of 13 rows)           5 failures
    perrow       (a tall picture one row for each key)           2 failures
    eolstop      (the mark under the picture is a stop)          9 failures
    twopress     (a picture that fits is scrolled like a tall)   2 failures
    nokeyabort   (a key does not end a glide)                    1 failure

6. OPEN POINTS
--------------
  - The original's own behaviour for a picture is not documented in the
    repository notes (docs/ORIGINAL-EDITOR-GAPS.txt names the picture marker
    only); the rules above follow the owner's wish, not a measurement of the
    original.
  - The mouse (W31) will need the same rule: a click on a row of the image
    puts the cursor on the picture.
