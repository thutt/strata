THE THESAURUS
=============

Status: done.  F12, Tools, Thesaurus and Ctrl-T look the word of the cursor up
in the MyThes data of the package mythes-en-us (an optional prerequisite, see
README.txt).  The original's behaviour was probed in Manuscript 2.1 under
DOSBox-X twice: a first probe for the design and a second one, listed below,
for the keys, the word finder and the effects on the document.  What was seen
is marked [V], what the help file says [H], what is a guess [?].  Few guesses
are left: they are in section 9.

1. The original
---------------
Tools, Thesaurus and Ctrl-T do the same.  The original needs no set-up under
DOSBox: american.syn is in C:\MS and the lookup works at once.  The help
file (topic "Thesaurus") says: if the word is in the thesaurus, synonyms are
displayed; Enter replaces the word in the document, Ctrl-T looks up the
highlighted synonym, Esc leaves without changing the document; if the word is
not there a list of similarly spelled words is shown.

The word.  [V] The word the cursor is in is looked up: with the cursor at the
start of "section", in the middle ("sec|tion") and just after its last letter
the lookup was "section"; with the cursor at the start of the next word it
was that word.  The word is highlighted in the text while the panel is open.
A cursor on a space or in a gap of spaces, on a marker, at the end of a line
after a full stop or after trailing spaces looks BACK over what is not a
letter, on the same line, to the word before it [V].  A cursor with no word
before it on its line (an empty line, a line that starts with spaces and the
cursor in them) gives the message "No word selected" [V]: a box titled
"Thesaurus" with the text and "Press Any Key to Continue".  Letters, an
apostrophe or a hyphen between letters make one word ("don't", "re-enter" were
looked up whole) [V]; digits are not part of a word ("123": the word before
was used) [V].  An attribute change ends a word: in "sec" (bold) "tion"
(normal) the cursor in the first part looks up "sec" and in the second part
"tion", and the highlight covers that part only [V].  A marker ends a word as
well: before[M]happy[M]after has three words, and with the cursor on a marker
the word before it is used [V].  A word of 80 letters that wrapped onto a
second screen row, with the cursor at the start of that row, was highlighted
only for the part on that row [V]; Strata always takes the whole word (up to
63 letters).

Length.  The help file has "Word is too long - maximum length is 16
characters" [H].  It could not be provoked: words of 16, 17, 19 and 80 letters
in the text and of 20 and 70 letters typed in the Look Up box were looked up
without an error and gave the list of similar words [V].  Strata has no
such error either (its limit is 63 letters: a longer word is cut off).

The panel.  [V] It fills 12 rows of the 25 row screen, full width, and the
text stays visible and is not scrolled.  If the cursor is on screen row 12 or
above (row 0 is the status line) the panel is the bottom 12 rows (13 to 24, it
hides the key line); if the cursor is on row 13 or below the panel is the TOP
12 rows (0 to 11) [V].  Top border with the title "WORD FINDER from
Microlytics, Inc." (the name of the licensed program).  Row 1: the word as it
was typed and a colon ("sections:", "Quick:"), at column 2, and at the right
MORE: with an arrow up, down or both when there is more to see.  Then 9 rows
of senses, one block each:

    noun - area, district, domain, environs, jurisdiction, locality,
           neighborhood, parish, precinct, province, range, region, sphere,
           vicinity, ward, zone;
         - citation, clipping, excerpt, extract, mention, passage, portion,
           quotation, quote, reference;
     adj - bent, committed, dedicated, determined, fixed, intentional;

A part of speech (noun, adj, verb, adv, prep; right aligned in columns 2 to 5)
is written at the first of its senses only; every sense starts with "-" in
column 7, the words begin in column 9 and are separated by ", ", a sense ends
with ";" and the last one with ".", and lines wrap before column 78 with the
words under the first.  The same word can be in several senses.  The first
word is highlighted (reverse video).  The bottom border holds the hint
"Enter:replace  Esc:exit  CTRL-T:look up", centred, and once a word has been
looked up from the panel "Ctrl+PgUp:last word" as well [V].  Only synonyms are
shown: no antonyms, generic or related terms.  A headword that has only
such terms gives th_lookup the value 2 and the panel the title "The word has
no synonyms -  Please choose another word" (the owner chose this on
2026-11-19; before, it said "not found").

Keys.  [V] Right and Left: next and previous word, over the ends of the rows
(no wrap at the very first or last word).  Down and Up: a row down or up, to
the word whose first column is nearest to the current word's.  Home and End:
the first and last word of the row.  PgDn: the next 9 rows (no overlap; the
highlight goes to the first word of the new top row; on the last page the
rows left are shown with blank rows below); PgUp: the same back.  The MORE
arrows follow the rows above and below the page.  Ctrl-Home highlights the
heading word ("Quick:", Enter would then put the same word back), Ctrl-End the
last word that is on the screen; Ctrl-PgDn does nothing [V].  Enter:
replace.  Ctrl-T: look up the highlighted word; the word that was shown is
kept in a history.  Ctrl-PgUp: back to the last word of the history (the
highlight is on the first word again; the hint "Ctrl+PgUp:last word" stays as
long as the history is not empty); without a history it does nothing.  Esc:
leave the thesaurus altogether, also from a second lookup.  A letter typed
opens a box "Look Up: h_" with the letter in it; the box is 3 rows high at
panel row 4, from column 7 to column 72, its bottom border has the hint
"Esc:exit  CTRL-T:look up", the panel stays as it was behind it.  Letters are
added, Backspace takes one off, Enter or Ctrl-T looks the word up, Esc closes
the box only and returns to the panel [V].  A word of 70 letters can be typed.

Not found.  [V] The panel is titled "The word was not found -  Please choose
another word": ten words of the dictionary in one column at column 21, in
alphabetical order, with the nearest word before the one looked up
highlighted and 5 words above it (4 below it): for "abcdefghijklmnopqrs" the
list is abatement ... abduct, highlight abbreviated, for "sec" it is
seasick ... seclusion, highlight seaworthy; at the start of the dictionary
the list begins with the first word and has ten words, at the end it ends with
the last word (for "zzzqx" only zoom and zygote, highlight zygote).  MORE:
shows up and down arrows and the keys move through the whole dictionary: Up
and Down one word (the list scrolls by a word at the edge), PgUp and PgDn the
ten words of a page, with the highlight at the same place of the list.  Enter
or Ctrl-T looks up the highlighted word, typing starts a new lookup, Esc
leaves.  Hint: "Enter:look up  Esc:exit  CTRL-T:look up" (and the
Ctrl+PgUp part when there is a history). 

Inflections.  [V] "headlines" and "sections" are found (the entry of the
singular is shown under the heading as typed); "ran" is not found; "running"
is found with noun and adjective senses only.

Replacing.  [V] Enter puts the highlighted word in place of the word in the
text and the cursor goes after the new word (the position counter shows the
column after the new word); the document is marked changed (the "*" before
the file name).  The case of the old word is kept: Quick gives Center, QUICK
gives CENTER, quick gives center; a synonym of two words ("next day") in
place of Quick gives "Next day" (only the first letter).  The new word is not
inflected: "sections" became "area".  The attributes of the word stay: a bold
word replaced by another one is bold in the file (the attribute change codes
stay where they were, the new letters are between them) [V].  Esc changes
nothing: no "*", the cursor is where it was and the highlight is gone [V].
There is no undo in the original (and none in Strata) [?]: the way back is
another replacement.  After the replacement the next Ctrl-T looks up the
cursor's word again, which is the new word.


2. The MyThes data
------------------
Two ASCII files, /usr/share/mythes/th_en_US_v2.idx (3.0 MB) and
th_en_US_v2.dat (18.5 MB).  Both start with a line naming the character
set, "UTF-8".  The data seen on this machine (Debian 13, package
1:25.2.3-1) contains no byte above 127, but a reader has to cope with UTF-8.

th_en_US_v2.idx
    line 1  UTF-8
    line 2  the number of entries: 145866
    then    one line per entry: headword | decimal byte offset into the dat
    The headwords are in plain byte order (checked: strictly increasing, no
    duplicates), all lower case, 63822 of them have a space, the longest
    has 71 characters.  Examples: success|16063530, section|14622685.

th_en_US_v2.dat
    line 1  UTF-8
    entry   headword | n        (n senses follow)
            (pos)|word|word|...  one line per sense
    A sense line starts with (noun), (verb), (adj) or (adv).  The words are
    synonyms of the headword, and a word followed by a space and one of
    these in parentheses is another kind of relation: "(generic term)"
    (358978 times in the file), "(similar term)" (53027), "(antonym)"
    (13060) and "(related term)" (10672).  Lines are up to 8.5 KB.
    section|14
    (noun)|subdivision|writing (generic term)|written material (generic
    term)|...  and set|45 has (adj)|fit|primed|ready (similar term).

Look-up.  The offset in the idx line is where the entry starts in the dat,
so a look-up is: binary search of the idx for the headword, then one read at
the offset (the headword line, then n sense lines).  The dat must not be
read as a whole.  The words are lower case, so a word typed in any case is
lower cased first.  There is no stemming in the files (sections, sets, ran
are not headwords); libmythes asks a spell checker's morphology for it.
Strata does not link libmythes (C++); it needs a small stemmer of its own
(section 4).  The character set of the editor is CP437; the words with
letters above 127 are not found in this data, and a UTF-8 word read from
a newer file is converted to CP437 with the table in term.c, a word with a
letter that has no CP437 form is dropped.

3. What is shown
----------------
The original shows synonyms only.  From a sense line Strata shows the words
without a suffix, then those marked "(similar term)" (MyThes puts the
members of an adjective cluster there and they are what a writer expects
to see); "(antonym)", "(related term)" and "(generic term)" are not shown,
and a sense that has nothing to show is left out.  A word that has no sense
left is treated as not found [?].  The parts of speech are written noun,
verb, adj and adv, in the order of the file.  A word is shown once in a
sense but may be in several.  MyThes has far more words than american.syn
(327 KB): "unnumbered" is not in the original and is in MyThes, so the
results differ from the original's.

4. The modules src/thesaurus.c and src/thesmenu.c
-------------------------------------------------
src/thesaurus.[ch] is the data layer; it knows nothing of the editor or the
screen.

    typedef struct {
        char pos[8];        /* "noun", "verb", "adj" or "adv" */
        char **words;       /* CP437, no suffixes, in file order */
        int nwords;
    } ThSense;

    typedef struct {
        char head[TH_HEAD]; /* the entry that was found, lower case */
        ThSense *senses;
        int nsenses;
    } ThEntry;

    void th_use(const char *path);   /* for the tests; NULL: the usual search */
    int  th_available(void);
    const char *th_path(void);       /* the .dat file in use */
    int  th_lookup(const char *word, ThEntry *out);
        /* 1 found (under its stem if needed), 0 not found, -1 no data. */
    void th_free(ThEntry *e);
    int  th_neighbours(const char *word, int step, int count, int mark_in,
                       char names[][TH_HEAD], int *mark);
    int  th_stem(const char *word, int try, char out[TH_HEAD]);
    void th_match_case(const char *orig, const char *repl, char *out,
                       int cap);

Files.  STRATA_THESAURUS names the data: a directory, the .dat file, or the
path without .idx and .dat; a directory is searched for th_en_US_v2,
th_en_US_new, th_en_US and th_test and then for any other .dat that has an
.idx.  When the variable is set, nothing else is tried: an empty or wrong
value means "not available", which the tests use.  When it is not set the
directory /usr/share/mythes is searched.  Both files are opened read only and
mapped (PROT_READ, MAP_PRIVATE): the system loads only the pages that are
touched, so memory stays small and a lookup costs a few page reads.  The
index is searched by bisecting on byte positions: go to the middle, back to
the start of that line, compare the headword (up to the "|") with the key on
unsigned bytes, and so on.  Nothing is parsed up front, so the first lookup
costs microseconds and the editor never waits.  A line of the index or the
data that does not have the expected shape, an offset that is past the end of
the data or does not hold the headword, or fewer sense lines than the count
give "not found" instead of a crash.  The reader checks the character set of
the first line: with UTF-8 the words of the data are converted to CP437 (a
word that has a letter that CP437 does not have is dropped) and the key of a
lookup is made in UTF-8.

Stemming.  The candidates are tried in this order and the first one that is a
headword with something to show wins: the word; with "'s" taken off; "ies" and
"ied" to "y"; "es" off; "s" off (not "ss"); "ed" off, and "ed" to "e"; "ing"
off, and "ing" to "e"; "er", "est" and "ly" off; each of the "ed", "ing",
"er" and "est" results also with a doubled last consonant undone (running to
run).  The original found sections, headlines and not ran, so irregular forms
are not handled.  The heading shows the word as typed.

The list of similar words.  th_neighbours finds the last headword that is not
after the word (the word itself if it is a headword), moves it by step
headwords (a page or a single word; clamped at both ends), and returns count
names that begin mark_in headwords before it, or at the first headword.  The
index is in byte order, which is what the original's list is in.  There is no
edit distance: the original's "similarly spelled" words are the neighbours in
the alphabetical order, and that is what the probes showed.  The cost is one
bisection and a few line steps.

src/thesmenu.[ch] is the panel; the only entry is thesmenu_lookup(Editor *e,
int cursor_row).

5. The panel
------------
The panels of widgets.c (popup_form, popup_menu, popup_grid) are for forms and
lists and do not fit: this one is drawn like the F12 menu of menu.c, with
term_puts and term_box (box() of widgets.c) on 12 rows, over the editor that
the callback w_redraw (the same one the popups use) paints.  The panel is the
bottom 12 rows when the cursor is on screen row rows-13 or above, else the top
12 rows; the text is not scrolled (section 1).  On a screen of fewer than 25
rows the panel has rows/2 rows.  The word is highlighted in the text with the
selection of the editor (SEL_TEXT from the start to the end of the word, the
cursor at the end) while the panel is open; the selection is cancelled when it
closes and, unless a word was put in, the cursor is put back.

State: the word that was looked up and its ThEntry, a table of the words
(row, first column, end column, the mark after it, the text) made when the
panel is laid out, the row where each sense starts, the highlighted word, the
first row shown and a history of looked up words (at most 20).  Layout as in
section 1.  The not-found panel has its own list (the names that
th_neighbours returns) and the keys of section 1.  Typing a letter, in either
panel, opens the "Look Up:" box.  The title is "Thesaurus", not "WORD FINDER
from Microlytics, Inc.", as the data is not Microlytics'; the title of the
not-found panel is the original's.  Keys that the panel does not know are
ignored.  Ctrl-PgUp needs a terminal that sends it (xterm does, ESC [ 5 ; 5 ~).

6. The word under the cursor, and the replacement
-------------------------------------------------
ed_word_at(e, &from, &to, buf, cap) in editor.c.  Text is a vector of tokens
(msdoc.h): a letter is TK_CHAR with the CP437 code in a.  A word is a run of
letters (A-Z, a-z and the CP437 letters 80 to A5) with an apostrophe or a
hyphen allowed between two letters.  Attribute tokens, tabs, markers and the
end of line end it, so a word is never split over one.  The cursor (Editor
cur: item, col, tok) is in the column text ed_text(e).  The word is the run
that holds token cur.tok; if that is not a letter, the run before it, found
by looking back over the tokens that are not letters up to the end of the
previous line (the word of the line only); if there is none the function
returns 0 and the caller says "No word selected".  A word over 63 letters
(THES_MAXWORD) is cut off.

ed_replace_range(e, from, to, text, n) in editor.c deletes the tokens
from..to-1, inserts one TK_CHAR for each byte, sets the cursor to from+n and
calls touch(), so that the block and the document are marked changed.  No
attribute token is inserted or removed: the attribute in force at the old word
applies to the new characters because they take the old place in the token
stream.  th_match_case makes the case: all capitals with two letters or more
make every letter of the replacement a capital; a capital first letter makes
the first letter a capital; otherwise it is as the data has it.  A synonym of
several words is inserted with ordinary spaces.  No inflection is added.

7. Where it is hooked in
------------------------
src/ui.c.  Case 20 of handle_key (Ctrl-T) and the entry Tools, Thesaurus (path
TOP_TOOLS, TOOLS_THESAURUS) call do_thesaurus, which gives thesmenu_lookup the
screen row of the cursor.  The entry is live whether or not the data is
there: without it a popup says "The thesaurus is not installed: add the
package mythes-en-us" and the editor goes on as before (the design had a
dimmed entry; a popup says what to do, which is better).  Also: Makefile
(thesaurus.o in EDOBJ, thesmenu.o in UIOBJ, the headers in the dependency
list), editor.c and editor.h (ed_word_at, ed_replace_range), docs, README.txt,
tests.  The prerequisite is listed in README.txt.

8. Tests
--------
The tests do not need the package.  tests/mkthesdata.py writes the small
dictionary tests/data/th_test.idx and th_test.dat in MyThes format (30
headwords, offsets computed): quick (a noun, an adjective with a similar term,
an antonym, a generic term and a related term, an adverb, a sense with nothing
to show), section (three senses, a synonym of several words), happy (an
antonym), headline, run, set, a headword of several words, and words around
"abcd" and at the end of the alphabet for the not-found list.  The README in
tests/data says more.

 - tests/edtest.c (make test): th_lookup (the senses, the dropped relations,
   the order, stems: sections, HEADLINES, running; ran not found), th_stem,
   th_neighbours (the list, the ends, steps), th_match_case, damaged data (a
   wrong head, an offset past the end, an offset into a line, fewer lines than
   the count, an empty dictionary, an empty path, a wrong path, the three ways
   of naming the data), ed_word_at (start, middle and end of a word, the next
   word, apostrophe, hyphen, digits, the end of a line, a long word, empty
   lines, an attribute, a marker) and ed_replace_range (attributes kept,
   cursor, modified flag, text).
 - tests/smoke.py (make smoke): the panel on a pty with STRATA_THESAURUS set
   to tests/data: Ctrl-T and Tools, Thesaurus, the layout, the highlight, the
   keys (Right, Left, Up, Down, Home, End), Ctrl-T on a synonym, Ctrl-PgUp, Esc,
   Enter (the file saved: Center, CENTER, center, "Next day"), Esc leaves
   everything as it was, the not-found list (highlight, Enter, Up, Down, PgUp,
   PgDn), the Look Up box (letters, Backspace, Enter, Ctrl-T, Esc), "No word
   selected", the panel above or below the cursor, no data (a popup, and the
   editor works) and one check with the real data of mythes-en-us (the
   directory in STRATA_TEST_MYTHES, else /usr/share/mythes; skipped with a
   message when it is not there).

9. Open questions and risks
---------------------------
 - Licence of the data.  The package copyright file (Debian) names the
   LibreOffice dictionaries project and, for dictionaries/en/*, a permissive
   notice by Kevin Atkinson; no line names the thesaurus data itself, which is
   derived from WordNet.  Strata only reads the files that the user installed
   and does not copy any of them (the test dictionary is written by us), but
   the licence should be read before the files are ever distributed with
   Strata.
 - Differences from the original: a far larger vocabulary, different senses and
   word lists (MyThes puts the members of an adjective cluster in "similar
   terms", which are shown after the synonyms), the nearest words of the not
   found list include phrases (63822 of the headwords have a space), stems by
   our own rules, no Microlytics title, no "too long" error.
 - Not tried in the original: the Home and End keys of the Look Up box; what
   Enter does in the not-found panel on a phrase (Strata looks it up); a word
   with letters above 127; an Alt-key in the panel; the panel on a screen of
   another size (Strata uses rows/2 rows under 25 rows).
 - The data may change with the package: the idx and dat of other releases may
   have another name (th_en_US_new) or contain UTF-8; the reader tolerates both
   and checks the first line of each file.
 - mmap of an 18 MB file is cheap, but a file that is replaced while Strata
   runs gives wrong text: the reader checks offsets against the size it saw and
   gives "not found" for anything that does not fit.
 - A sense list of 45 senses (set) has hundreds of words; the table is built
   for the whole entry once, which is small, and only the rows on the screen
   are drawn.  At most 64 senses of an entry are kept.
