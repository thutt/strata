THE VT100 CHARACTER SET OF THE TERMINAL PROGRAMS
================================================

Files: src/term.c (choose_charset, the tables cp437_acs and cp437_ascii,
term_present), src/config.c and src/str.c (the setting and the Setup field),
tests/vt100.py, tests/tui.py (the screen model), tests/termcheck.py.

1. THE THREE SCREENS

The terminal programs (stred, str) draw their screen in one of three ways.
The choice is made once per run, when the terminal is opened.

  utf8    The shapes of the original character set (cp437_uni in
          src/layout.c): double and single lines, shades, blocks, triangles,
          arrows.  Every shape is written as its UTF-8 bytes.  The terminal
          must read UTF-8 and its font must have the shapes.
  vt100   Only ASCII bytes are written (no byte of 0x80 or more).  The
          lines, the checkerboard, the diamond, the bullet and the arrows
          are the letters of the DEC Special Graphics set, written between
          ESC ( 0 and ESC ( B; everything else is ASCII.  The terminal does
          not need UTF-8, a locale or a font with box characters.
  ascii   Only ASCII bytes, with plain look-alikes (+ - | = # and so on).

The X11 programs are not changed: they draw every cell with their own fonts
and do not use the three screens.

The setting terminal_charset (Setup, Terminal Characters) takes auto (the
field says Automatic, and no line is written to the file), utf8, vt100 and
ascii.  The variable STRATA_ASCII=1 forces ascii and STRATA_ASCII=0 forces
utf8; it wins over the setting.

2. THE MAPPING

The program keeps its screen as cells of a CP437 character (term_put) or a
Unicode character (term_putu).  Only the output differs.  In vt100 the table
cp437_acs of src/term.c gives, for each shape of the CP437 set, the letter of
the graphics set, or says "block", or says "none", and then the ASCII
look-alike of cp437_ascii is used, as in ascii.  Every character is one
column wide.

  Shape (CP437 number)            UTF-8      vt100            ascii
  ------------------------------  ---------  ---------------  -------------
  single line corners, tees and   as drawn    the same shape:  + - |
  cross (B3 B4 BF C0 to C5 D9 DA)            j k l m n t u v
                                             w x
  double lines and the mixed      as drawn   the single line  + = |
  ones (B5 to BE, C6 to D8, BA               of the same
  BB BC C8 to CF, CD)                        shape (the set has
                                             no double lines)
  horizontal line C4 and CD       as drawn   q               - and =
  vertical line B3 and BA         as drawn   x               |
  shades B0 B1 B2                 as drawn   a (checker-     : : #
                                             board)
  blocks DB to DF and FE          as drawn   a space in the  #
                                             reverse attribute
  triangles 10 11 1E 1F, arrows   as drawn   + , - . (the    > < ^ v
  18 19 1A 1B                                arrows of the
                                             set: right,
                                             left, up, down)
  the arrows 12 and 1D (two-way)  as drawn   ASCII           | and -
  diamond 04                      as drawn   ` (diamond)     *
  bullet 07, centered dots F9 FA  as drawn   ~ (centered     * and .
                                             dot)
  accented letters, Greek letters, as drawn  the ASCII       the same
  math signs, currency signs, and            look-alike
  the other shapes (faces, cards,            (e for an e
  notes)                                     with an accent)
  a character with no CP437 form  none       ?               ?

Decisions:
  - The arrows of the set (+ , - .) are used for the triangles of the lists
    and the markers and for the arrows, because every VT100 has them; the
    other shapes that the set lacks keep their ASCII look-alike.
  - A block is a space in the reverse attribute (the attribute that the cell
    has, with reverse added), so that it is a filled cell on every terminal
    whatever the set has at the letter 0.  The half blocks (DC to DF) are
    drawn as a full cell.
  - The set has the accented letters of no language.  A character of a
    DOCUMENT that is not ASCII is drawn as the ASCII look-alike of ascii
    mode (reused: cp437_ascii) and is never lost from the document: the
    screen is only a view, the text in memory and in the file is unchanged.
  - Where the CP437 text of a document has a line or box shape (the table of
    characters of the sample chars.doc), that shape is drawn as a line of
    the set too, because the cell that is drawn has no mark of where the
    shape came from.  The task asked for the ASCII look-alike there; it was
    not built, and the case is rare.
  - The letters of the set that the screen does not use (the degree sign,
    plus-minus, the signs of order, pi, the sterling sign) are not used: the
    degree sign and the others of a document are drawn as their ASCII
    look-alike.

3. THE DETECTION (auto)

The reasons are written in the log of the run (str) as the choice always
was: "term: the screen is drawn in VT100, not in UTF-8: <reason>".  That is a
warning, so that a release build records it; a choice by the variable or the
setting, and UTF-8, are notes, which only debug builds record.  The order:

  (a) STRATA_ASCII (1 ascii, 0 utf8), then the setting terminal_charset
      (utf8, vt100, ascii).  Nothing is asked of the terminal.
  (b) In a multiplexer (TMUX or STY is set, or TERM begins with screen or
      tmux): UTF-8 when the locale is UTF-8, else vt100 when the entry of
      TERM has the line characters, else ascii.  A multiplexer translates
      the characters itself, so what the terminal behind it does is not what
      the program writes to; the locale (that of the multiplexer) and the
      type of terminal that the multiplexer shows are what counts.  Nothing
      is asked of the terminal.
  (c) A locale that is UTF-8: the terminal is asked (the probe: U+2502 is
      written at a known cell and the cursor position is requested; one
      column means a terminal that reads UTF-8).  One column, or no answer
      in 800 ms, gives utf8.  An answer of more than one column goes on to
      (d).
  (d) A locale that is not UTF-8, or the answer of the probe was more than
      one column: vt100 when the terminal can draw it, else ascii (e).  The
      probe is NOT asked when the locale is not UTF-8: the answer could not
      change the choice (utf8 needs the locale, too), and a terminal that is
      not in UTF-8 would show the three bytes of the probe as odd
      characters.  With that, vt100 writes no byte of 0x80 or more at all.
      The terminal can draw VT100 when
        1. the entry of TERM in the terminal database has the string acsc
           (acs_chars) with the letters of the set at themselves (jj kk ll
           mm nn qq tt uu vv ww xx), or
        2. the entry is missing or has no such string, and the terminal
           answers the question who it is (ESC [ c, waiting 100 ms at most,
           and only when the input is a terminal) with a VT100 or a later
           model: ESC [ ? 1 ; 2 c is a VT100, 6 a VT102, and 62 to 69 the
           VT220 and later models.  The answers 4, 7 and 12 (VT132, VT131,
           VT125) are taken too.  No answer, or another answer, is no.
      The query is made only when the entry of TERM does not decide.

The choice that the program made by itself leaves a notice for the first
screen: "No UTF-8 terminal: VT100 lines are drawn. See Setup,
terminal_charset." or "No UTF-8 or VT100 terminal: ASCII is drawn. See Setup,
terminal_charset."  A choice by the variable or by the setting leaves none.

The terminal database is read from the files of the terminfo folders in the
order that the library of the system uses (TERMINFO, the folder .terminfo of
the home, TERMINFO_DIRS, /etc/terminfo, /lib/terminfo, /usr/share/terminfo,
/usr/lib/terminfo), because the library (ncurses) is not linked: src/term.c
reads the compiled entry itself (the string number 146, acs_chars).  A
machine with no database folder at all uses the names xterm, rxvt, vt100,
vt102, vt220, screen, tmux, st, alacritty, kitty, foot, wezterm and putty
(also with a hyphen and a suffix).  A type that is in the database without
the capability (dumb, ansi) is asked who it is.  The entry of ansi has the
string, but its letters are bytes of the PC set, not of the DEC set, so it is
not taken.

A failed or absent answer never holds up the start: the wait is 100 ms for
the question who it is, and the probe keeps its 800 ms as before.

4. HOW THE SET IS SWITCHED

  - A run of cells that are lines is written with one ESC ( 0, the letters,
    and ESC ( B before the next cell that is not a line.
  - The cells are compared as the program draws them (the character and the
    attribute): the checkerboard and the letter a are different cells, as
    are a block and a space, so that a cell is redrawn when its look
    changes.
  - Every update ends in ASCII, and the set is switched off before every
    move of the cursor (ESC [ row ; column H), because a terminal may take
    the next bytes for a set change.  The cursor is shown after that.
  - ESC ( B is the first thing of the sequence that is written at the exit,
    at a signal that ends the program (SIGHUP, SIGINT, SIGQUIT, SIGTERM,
    SIGSEGV, SIGBUS, SIGABRT, SIGFPE, SIGILL, by the handler), and before a
    child program runs (the Document Manager runs the Editor, the Viewer and
    the others after term_close).  It is written when the terminal is opened,
    too, in case the terminal was left in the set.
  - Ctrl-Z is the key Undo of the Editor (the terminal is in raw mode), so
    the program is not suspended by the key.  A stop by a signal from
    outside leaves the terminal in ASCII, because every update ends in it.
  - A program that is killed by SIGKILL cannot restore anything: the same as
    for the colour of the cursor and the alternate screen.

5. LIMITS

  - No double lines and no shades other than the checkerboard: the set has
    none.  The double-line frames of the original are single lines.
  - No accented letters: they are drawn as the letter without the accent.
  - A terminal that lies in TERM (a type that has the capability on a
    terminal that has not) draws letters where the lines belong; the cure is
    the setting ascii.  A terminal that lacks the arrows of the set (they are
    in the VT100, but not every emulator draws them) shows + , - . in place
    of the arrows; the setting ascii shows > < ^ v.
  - Nothing here is a test of the fonts of the terminals: the lines of the
    set are drawn by the terminal itself, the arrows by its font.

6. HOW IT WAS TESTED

tests/vt100.py (in the smoke list) runs str and stred in the pty of
tests/tui.py, whose screen model knows ESC ( 0 and ESC ( B and maps the
letters to the shapes (so that the existing screen checks see the shapes),
and has the steps gfx (the letters of the cells written in the set),
signal:NAME and rawbytes (the bytes as they were written).  The checks:

  - TERM=xterm, LANG=C, no answer: the menu has the single-line box with its
    title and text; the letters l k m j q x are the cells in the set; the
    text is not; no byte of 0x80 or more in the whole session (menu, Setup);
    one ESC ( 0 for a run; no empty switch; the set is off at the end of
    every update, before every move and at the end; no probe and no question
    who it is; the notice names the setting; the log names the reason.
  - The Editor: a document of ASCII text, the same with an accented letter
    typed (drawn as e, kept as the byte 82, saved as after the same keys on
    the UTF-8 screen), the table of the characters.
  - After SIGTERM the bytes of the handler begin with ESC ( B and leave the
    alternate screen; after Ctrl-Z; after Quit; before the output of a child
    program (a shell script that the Document Manager runs in place of the
    Editor).
  - UTF-8: LANG=en_US.UTF-8 with the probe answered by one column gives the
    double lines; with no answer it does too; with the answer of three
    columns it gives vt100.
  - The setting vt100, utf8 and ascii force their mode whatever the terminal
    and the locale say; STRATA_ASCII wins over the setting; auto and an
    unknown value are the automatic choice.
  - An unknown TERM with no answer gives ascii in less than 4 seconds; the
    answers of a VT100 and of a VT220 give vt100; another answer gives
    ascii; TERM=xterm is not asked; TERM=dumb gives ascii; TERM=linux gives
    vt100; a description in a folder that TERMINFO names is read.
  - A multiplexer (TMUX in the environment): TERM=screen with a locale that
    is not UTF-8 gives vt100, an unknown type gives ascii, a UTF-8 locale
    gives utf8; nothing is asked in any of them.

tests/termcheck.py (the start check, in the smoke list) was brought up to
date: the cases that expected ASCII from a locale C and a type of terminal
that xterm has now expect vt100; the ASCII cases use TERM=dumb; the Setup
test knows the new value (Automatic, UTF-8, VT100, ASCII).

7. WHAT XTERM SHOWS

Not checked again for this work: xterm is not installed on the machine on
which the work was done (alien), so no xterm was run under Xvfb and no
picture of an xterm was taken.  What is known comes from earlier work:

  - The pictures of the sample that came with the order (the stand-alone
    program vt100menu.c, not part of the repository), made in an xterm with
    LC_ALL=C: the single-line box of the VT100 set is drawn right with no
    option and no font setting; and the double lines of the UTF-8 screen
    fall apart in the same xterm (each box character shows as a letter with
    a circumflex and other signs, the box is gone).
  - docs/TERMINALS.txt has the probes of xterm 407 of an earlier machine
    (the table of section 3): the outcomes there are what was observed then;
    they were not repeated.  The owner said that the statements of the
    manual about xterm were wrong; the manual no longer calls xterm the
    recommended terminal and no longer gives the options as facts (see
    manual/src/gs-need.src).
  - Not observed, and so not claimed: the effect of the options -lc, -u8,
    -fa and -fs, of the resources utf8, locale and faceName, and of the
    metaSendsEscape resource, in this version; and the arrows of the set in
    xterm.

To check a terminal yourself (also written in the manual): the command
printf '\033(0lqqk\033(B\n' prints the top of a box if the terminal speaks
VT100, and printf '\342\224\200\n' prints a horizontal line if it reads UTF-8
and has the shape in its font.
