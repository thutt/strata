





          1 Project Issues and Approach
          The proposed development of Centennial Park offers the citizens
          of Central City a valuable opportunity to take an unused and
          unsightly city space and turn it into open space with both pas-
          sive and active recreational facilities. The 10-acre site pres-
          ents numerous possibilities, but it also offers some technical
          challenges that must be addressed to ensure the park is properly
          developed.

          In the last eight years, the site has been abandoned with only
          intermittent dumping of excavation fill and rubble. Prior use
          included heavier dumping of fill, but there is no record of the
          quantities or the makeup of the fill. There is also a portion of
          the site that never received any fill and that presents an
          entirely different soil and subsoil profile. The terrain includes
          slopes of the fill as well as this low-lying area and drainage
          has been a persistent problem. Finally, the neighborhood resi-
          dents have, for many years, used portions of the site at will and
          without formal city involvement. The city's current plans are
          perceived by some neighbors as an intrusion.
          Because of the many facets of the project, CNS has selected expe-
          rienced geologists, hydrologists, landscape architects, and civil
          engineers from its well-rounded staff.  In addition, Woodward and
          Flint will provide special geotechnical expertise; and Parker and
          Chang, specialists in land-use planning and community relations,
          will act as consultants. This project team is well equipped to
          provide all services necessary to properly implement the project.

          CNS feels that the problems of the Centennial Park site must be
          handled sensitively and thoroughly. The rest of this section dis-
          cusses these issues in detail.

            1.1 Site Use and Development
            Several site issues affect the development of the park and are
            of particular concern.

               1.1.1 Soil Conditions

               On-site investigation reveals that the presence of clays,
               building rubble, steep slopes, and the lack of growing
               medium in washed out areas need to be addressed. Soil chem-
               istry evaluation and testing for potentially hazardous mate-
               rials will also be done.

               1.1.2 Settlement
               The portion of the site used by the city for dumping rubble
               from street excavation and other city projects presents the
               possibility of differential settlement. In addition, piping
               settlement on the playing field site is of special concern.
               Piping settlement tends to suddenly cause holes in a fin-
               ished playing field and other large grassed areas. Special
               attention to the subgrading of these areas is important.














               A settlement monitoring program will be instituted to estab-
               lish and continually update settlement rate information.
               This information, in turn, will be used to determine the
               location of any settlement-sensitive park features.

               1.1.3 Surface Drainage
               Again, the dumping area of the park site influences the
               surface water flow considerably and a system must be pro-
               vided to direct surface water away from the site to appro-
               priate storm drains. Studies indicate that without the extra
               surface water from the fill area, the existing storm drain
               in the lower lying portion of the site can provide adequate
               drainage. The drainage system will also be designed to pre-
               vent the soil erosion that is currently occurring on the
               slopes.

               1.1.4 Groundwater

               The RFP indicates that there is concern about the condition
               of the groundwater on the site. A thorough analysis of the
               groundwater will be implemented and a monitoring program
               established to make sure none of the previously dumped fill
               is affecting groundwater quality and that the development of
               the park does not affect the groundwater level.

          2 Scope of Work
          The following scope of work and task assignment information dem-
          onstrates our approach to the designing and development of the
          park.

            2.1 Phase 1: Data Collection and Analysis

            Collection of information about the current state of the site
            will include:
                * Field and photogrammetric survey and preparation of topo-
                  graphic map

                * Detailed survey of the area for final property lines,
                  verification of utility locations, location of monitoring
                  wells and settlement platforms, and existing drainage
                  culverts
                * Soil chemistry evaluation and testing for potentially
                  hazardous materials

                * Evaluation of subsurface soils to determine required
                  amendments to ensure plant growth
                * Settlement monitoring program established to determine
                  settlement rates

                * Evaluation of surface water and potential drainage pat-
                  terns, including evaluation of capacities of existing
                  storm drains in the area
                * Analysis of groundwater quality and level













                * Site use analysis with public meetings to determine needs
                * Analysis of vehicular and pedestrian circulation patterns
                  on and adjacent to the site to determine logical access
                  points, parking requirements, and police and fire access

                * Visual analysis of the site with respect to on-site and
                  off-site views, landform design, and overall park design
            Summary reports on these and any other initial investigations
            and analyses will be prepared for the city.

            2.2 Phase 2: Preliminary Design

            Based on the existing conditions and needs determined in Phase
            1, a preliminary design will be prepared including general con-
            cept, locations of activities, and landscape and engineering
            schematics that address the specific problems of the site.
            Alternatives to portions of the design will also be explored,
            as appropriate.
            Identification of the necessary permits and a permitting sched-
            ule will also be included in the preliminary plan.

            2.3 Phase 3: Final Design, Contracting, and Permitting

            Based on the approved preliminary design, we will prepare final
            design plans, specification, and contract documents. These will
            include:
                * Construction cost estimates

                * All park design features together with the proposed con-
                  struction details and technical specifications
                * Updated settlement, soil, and hydrological information

                * All legal, permitting, and administrative construction
                  contract documents, which will conform to city and state
                  requirements
            Upon final approval of the final design, we will print con-
            struction documents for bidding.

            2.4 Phase 4: Construction

            We assume the city will provide personnel who will review the
            site on a daily basis. We will:
                * Assist the city in securing, evaluating, and awarding
                  bids

                * Assist the city in construction administration
                * Make site visits during the construction period at the
                  city's request to discuss work in progress and review
                  completed work for conformance with plans.

            Alternatively, CNS can provide full-time resident inspection of
            the construction phase if the city so desires.














            2.5 Phase 5: Maintenance Program
            The monitoring of settlement and groundwater quality and level
            will continue through Phase 4 and beyond. CNS will develop a
            schedule for these monitoring programs. If the city requests,
            CNS will turn the maintenance tasks over to the city after one
            year, or the city can contract CNS to continue monitoring the
            site. We feel that at least one year of monitoring by CNS is
            necessary to provide continuity of these important programs.

          3 Community Issues

            3.1 Neighborhood Support

            The development of the park must be responsive to the needs of
            the neighbors as well as the city at large. Meetings will be
            held to ascertain the feelings of the community and to respond
            to these needs. Safety and security on the site as well as
            traffic, noise, lighting, and visual impact will all need to be
            addressed in public meetings.

            3.2 Park Security
            Police and fire department access to the site, fencing, and
            area lighting all will be investigated in the promotion of park
            security. Both the city and the neighbors will have input on
            this important topic so that all parties feel their concerns
            have been considered. Any pathways will accommodate police and
            maintenance vehicles. Special fencing to protect private prop-
            erty may be required in places.

            3.3 Parking and Traffic

            Parking requirements will depend on the access routes, park
            user type, and the method of transportation that park users
            choose. A thorough study involving communication with city and
            neighborhood groups will be completed before final determina-
            tion of the amount of parking necessary.
            Access to the park should be designed to minimize impact on the
            neighborhood. The reconstruction of Oak Drive will achieve this
            goal while at the same time maintaining acceptable traffic
            standards.

          4 Schedule and Budget

            4.1 Time Schedule

            The CNS team believes the project should be on a two-year
            schedule from the notice to proceed, with the final design
            accepted and bidding under way at the beginning of the second
            year. The actual dates will be dependent on the project's
            notice to proceed date and the duration of review periods.
















            4.2 Fee Schedule
            CNS proposes to enter into an acceptable contract with Central
            City that provides for compensation for services on the basis
            of Usual Day Rates. This type of contract is widely accepted in
            the engineering and municipal public works sector and provides
            equity and safeguards for both parties as compensation is made
            only for work performed.  A reasonable not-to-exceed ceiling
            based on an agreed upon scope of services would be negotiated
            with the city. The estimates follow.
