; Editing: copy, cut, paste, delete, append, split, join, search, go to.
= Copy Cut and Paste
~ Copy
~ Cut
~ Paste
~ Menu:Edit:Block:Copy
~ Menu:Edit:Block:Cut
~ Menu:Edit:Block:Paste
~ Menu:Edit:Block:Move
~ Move
{F3} copies, {F4} cuts and {F5} pastes.  {F7} moves: it cuts, and the next
{F5} puts the text in its new place.

After {F3} or {F4} choose what to work on, a unit of Text, a Column, a Block
or a Section (see [[Units of Text]]).  Move the cursor to highlight it and
press {Enter}.  {Esc} cancels.  The cut or copied text is kept; {F5} puts it in
front of the cursor, and can be used again and again.

- {Ctrl-X} cuts text, {Ctrl-K} cuts the block that holds the cursor, and
  {Ctrl-L} cuts the section.
- {Ctrl-F3}, {Ctrl-F4} and {Ctrl-F5} do the same with the [[Library]], which
  keeps named entries.

See [[Delete]], [[Search]].

= Border
~ Border Layout
~ Menu:Edit:Layout:Border
Alt-F8 puts lines around columns and blocks.  Highlight the column or columns
with the cursor keys and press {Enter}; the panel Border Layout opens.  All,
Outside, Top, Left, Inside, Right and Bottom are None, Single, Double or
Wide; All is every border, Outside the four outer sides, Inside the lines
between columns.  Top Gutter and Bottom Gutter are inches with up to three
decimals, Connect Borders? is Yes or No and Shading is a percent.  Ins uses the
settings that you changed, Shift-Ins every setting.
The highlight can cover several columns and several rows (blocks): the
cursor keys extend it as a rectangle, and the settings go to every column
in it.  Where the columns differ, a field shows n/a (or is empty) and
changes nothing.  Do a row, then the rows below it, to give a table
different borders; a line that two neighbours share is drawn once, and
the stronger one is the one that shows.  The screen shows the
borders at once, and the Printer draws them with closed corners and junctions,
the gutters, the shading and the spacing of Connect Borders.  Undo takes them
away.  See [[Print]].

= Math
~ Math of
~ Menu:Tools:Math
Ctrl-F2 calculates the numbers in a part of the document.  Choose what to
calculate, a Text, a Column, a Block or a Section (see [[Units of Text]]),
highlight it with the cursor keys and press {Enter}.  The result is on the
clipboard: Paste (F5) puts it in the text, before you cut or copy anything
else.  The message line shows Result: and the number.  The numbers are added
when no operator is between them; the operators are () ^ - * / + -, % divides by
100, e or E gives a power of ten, other characters are ignored.  The period is
the decimal point.  A division by zero is refused.  The document is not
changed.  See [[Case]].

= Case
~ Case of
~ Menu:Edit:Layout:Case
Ctrl-F6 changes the case of letters.  A box prompts for Upper (every letter a
capital), Lower (every letter a small letter) or Capitalize (the first letter
of each word a capital, the others small).  Then choose what to change, a Text,
a Column, a Block or a Section (see [[Units of Text]]), highlight it with the
cursor keys and press {Enter}.  {Esc} cancels.  With Text and nothing
highlighted, {Enter} changes the word under the cursor.

Only letters change. The letters with an accent that the character set has in
two cases change; the others, and the German sharp s, stay. {Ctrl-/} takes the
change back as one step. See [[Copy Cut and Paste]].

= Delete
~ Delete (Alt-F4)
~ Menu:Edit:Block:Delete
Alt-F4 deletes text, a column, a block or a section after you highlight it.  To
remove less, use a key:

- {Del} removes the character at the cursor, and {Backspace} the one before it.
- {Ctrl-D} removes the rest of the word, and {Alt-Backspace} the word before the
  cursor.
- {Ctrl-K} removes the block that holds the cursor, {Ctrl-L} the section.

At the end of the last line of a block, {Del} joins the next block to it.
See [[Copy Cut and Paste]], [[Join]].

= Append
~ Append (Alt-F2)
~ Menu:Edit:Parts:Append
Append adds something after the cursor.  {Alt-F2} opens the list:

- Column: a new column in the block.
- Block: a new block after this one, with the format of this block ({Ctrl-A}).
- Global-block: a new block with the global format ({Ctrl-O}).
- Peer-section: a new section at the same level.
- Sub-section: a new section below this one.

The last two are for a structured document.  {Ins} also starts a new section.
See [[Split]], [[Join]].

= Split
~ Split (F2)
~ Menu:Edit:Parts:Split
{F2} splits at the cursor.  The list offers Column, Block and, in a structured
document, Section.  {Ctrl-V} splits the block and {Ctrl-Y} splits the section.
See [[Append]], [[Join]].

= Join
~ Join (Alt-F3)
~ Menu:Edit:Parts:Join
{Alt-F3} joins what is split: Block puts the next block after the text of this
one, and Section (in a structured document) joins the next section.  If nothing
can be joined the message line shows so.  See [[Split]], [[Delete]].

= Overtype
~ Overtype (Alt-F5)
~ Menu:Edit:Insert:Overtype
{Alt-F5} turns Overtype on or off.  With Overtype on, what you type replaces
the characters under the cursor instead of pushing them to the right.  The
status line shows OVR while it is on.

= Set Attributes
~ Attributes
~ Menu:Edit:Layout:Attribute
{Alt-F6} opens a list: Normal, Bold, Italics and Underscore Words.  Each puts a
change of attribute into the text at the cursor; everything after it, up to the
next change, has the attribute.  The keys are quicker:

- {Ctrl-B} bold on or off.
- {Alt-I} italics on or off.
- {Ctrl-U} underscore on or off.
- {Ctrl-N} normal text: all off.

= Block Format Editor
~ Justification
~ Menu:Edit:Layout:Format
~ Indent
~ Format
{F6} (or {Ctrl-E}) changes the format of the block that holds the cursor.  The
panels follow one another:

- the left indent of the block, in ruler units;
- Justification: Left, Right, Center or Even;
- Indent: None, First, All but First or All;
- Attributes: Normal, Bold, Italics, Bold Italics or Underscore Words;
- Tabs: the tab stops, such as 5,20R,40D (0 for none).

{Esc} in any one cancels the change.  {Ctrl-H} sets a hanging indent: type the
characters that are to hang, a bullet and a space, then press it.

= Search
~ Search (F9)
~ Menu:Tools:Find:Search
~ Menu:Tools:Find:Again
~ Search:Search for
~ Search:Find
~ Search:Direction
~ Search:Match Case
~ Search:Whole Word
~ Search:Attribute
{F9} searches.  The panel prompts for what to Search for and what to Find (Text,
the next Print Error, a Tab, an End of Line, a Nonbreaking Space or a Marker),
the Direction, Forward or Backward, whether to Match Case, whether to match a
Whole Word and which Attribute the text must have (Any, Plain, Bold, Italics
or Underscore).  Match Case and Whole Word apply to Text only.  With no string
and an Attribute, the search goes to the start of the next text that has it.
{Ctrl-F9} repeats the search with the same settings.

The search goes round the document: from the end it goes on from the start
and the message line shows so.  The message line shows Found or Not found, and
what was looked for.  Search finds text; to find markers choose Marker in Find.
See [[Replace]], [[Print Errors]].

= Replace
~ Replace (Alt-F9)
~ Menu:Tools:Find:Replace
~ Replace:Search for
~ Replace:Replace with
~ Replace:Find
~ Replace:Match Case
~ Replace:Whole Word
~ Replace:Attribute
~ Replace:Replacement Case
~ Replace:Ask at Each
? N replacement
? Replace?
{Alt-F9} replaces text.  Type the text to search for, the text to put in its
place and the options: the same Match Case, Whole Word and Attribute as Search,
the Find (Text, Tab or Nonbreaking Space), the Replacement Case (As Typed, or
Follow Found, which gives the replacement the capitals of the text found) and
whether to Ask at Each place.

With Ask at Each Yes, Replace goes from the cursor, on from the start of the
document when it reaches the end, up to where it began, and stops at each place:
{R} replaces and goes on, {O} replaces and stops, {S} skips the place, {A}
replaces this place and all the others, {Q} or {Esc} stops.  With No it
replaces every place at once.  The message line shows how many were replaced.
{Ctrl-/} takes the whole Replace back.  See [[Search]].

= Go To
~ Go To (Alt-F7)
~ Menu:Tools:Find:Go To
{Alt-F7} jumps to a place: Document start, Document end, Section number (you
are asked for the number), Next section and Previous section.  See
[[Go To Section]].

= Go To Section
Type the number of a section, such as 2.1, and press {Enter}.  If there is no
such section the message line shows so.  The sections are those of a
[[Structured and Unstructured|structured document]].

= Table
~ Table (F8)
~ Menu:Edit:Layout:Table
{F8} opens the Table list: Add column puts a new column in the block, and Split
column here splits the column at the cursor.  A table is a block with several
columns, drawn side by side.  See [[Append]], [[Split]].

= Edit Menu
~ Menu:Edit
~ Menu:Edit:Title
~ Menu:Edit:Structured
~ Menu:Edit:Unstructured
~ Menu:Edit:Section
~ Menu:Edit:Document
~ Menu:Edit:Window
~ Menu:Edit:Block
~ Menu:Edit:Parts
~ Menu:Edit:Layout
~ Menu:Edit:Insert
The Edit menu depends on the document.

- Title edits the title page.
- Structured makes an unstructured document structured, and Unstructured does
  the reverse.
- Section edits only the section that holds the cursor, and Document brings
  back the whole document ({Ctrl-^} does either).
- Window shows two documents on the screen at once: Open, Close, Adjust,
  Zoom-up, Zoom-down and Switch.
- Block has Copy, Cut, Paste, Move and Delete; Parts has Split, Join and
  Append; Layout has Format, Table, Attribute, Border and Case; Insert has
  Marker and Overtype.  Each entry does what its function key does.

See [[Title Page]], [[Structured and Unstructured]].

= Title Page
~ Text Editor:Title Page
The title page is the text before the first section of a structured document.
Choose Title on the Edit menu to edit it.  See [[Page Layout]] for how it is
printed.

= Word Count
~ Menu:Tools:Count
~ Count
Count tells you how many words the document has, in a small box.  It is on the
Tools menu.

= Nonbreaking Spaces
~ Nonbreaking space
Press {Ctrl-Shift-Space} to type a space that does not let the line end.  Use it
between a title and a name, or a number and its unit.  On the screen it looks
like a small cap between the words, and it prints as a space.

= Soft Hyphens
~ Soft hyphen
~ Hyphenation, soft
Press {Alt-_} (Alt with Shift and the hyphen key) to say where a word may be
broken at the end of a line. Type it inside the word: pro{Alt-_}ject.  If the
word falls at the end of a line in the print, it is broken there and a hyphen
is printed; if not, the word is whole.  A word with a soft hyphen is broken
only where you said. On the screen it is a small dim triangle.  If your
terminal does not pass {Alt-_}, press {Ctrl-F} and choose Soft hyphen.  Find
with Soft Hyphen finds them, and a search for a word goes through them.  Words
that you want always broken in the same way can go into the hyphenation list:
see [[Maintain Dictionaries]].

= The Mouse
~ Mouse
~ Wheel
The mouse is used in the X11 window only; the terminal version does
not use it, and the terminal keeps its own selection and paste.  The
wheel scrolls the text by three rows, and by half a screen with
{Ctrl}; the cursor stays in the rows that are shown.  In a list and in
this help the wheel is three {Up} or {Down} keys.  A click with the
left button puts the cursor on the character under the mouse arrow;
while text is highlighted the click moves the end of the highlight.  A
click in a menu, in a box, on the status line or on the keys at the
foot is ignored, and the wheel does nothing in a menu.  Nothing can be
selected by dragging, and every command is still on a key.

= Typing Special Characters
~ Special characters
Text is made of the characters of the PC character set (code page 437), which
has the accented letters, the Greek letters and the box drawing characters.
Type a character with the key that your keyboard layout gives it.  Text that
comes from a file keeps its characters when you [[Import]] it with the right
character set.


= Text Editor
~ Text Editor
The text of a note, a comment, an equation and a title page is edited in a
text editor of its own, which fills the screen.  The status line names it:
NOTE EDIT, COMMENT EDIT, EQUATION EDIT or TITLE PAGE EDIT.  You type and edit
as on the [[Edit Screen]], and the same keys work.

- {Ins} keeps your changes and takes you back to the document.
- {Esc} throws the changes away and takes you back to the text as it was.  While
  text is highlighted, {Esc} cancels the highlight first, and a second {Esc}
  leaves.
- {F1} shows this help, or the help of the kind of text that you are editing:
  [[Note Text]], [[Comment Text]], [[Equation Text]] or [[Title Page]].

A note may have several paragraphs.  Nothing is changed in the document until
you press {Ins}.

= Note Text
~ Text Editor:Note
You are editing the text of a note: a footnote, a levelnote or an endnote.  The
text may have several paragraphs.  Press {Ins} to keep the text and {Esc} to
leave it as it was.  The [[Note Marker]] tells how the note is printed.  The
editor itself is described in [[Text Editor]].

= Comment Text
~ Text Editor:Comment
You are editing the text of a comment, a remark for yourself or for the people
who work on the document.  A comment is printed only in a Draft print.  Press
{Ins} to keep the text and {Esc} to leave it as it was.  See [[Comment Marker]]
and [[Text Editor]].

= Equation Text
~ Text Editor:Equation
You are editing the text of an equation, written in TeX math.  Press {Ctrl-W}
to see the equation as it will be typeset, {Ins} to keep the text and {Esc} to
leave it as it was.  See [[Writing Equations]], [[Equation Marker]] and
[[Text Editor]].
