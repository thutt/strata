KNOWN ISSUES OF STRATA

This document lists the faults and the limits that the project knows and has
decided not to fix, with the reason for each decision.  A fault that is being
fixed is not listed here: it is in docs/WORKLIST.txt.  Every entry says what
the issue is, where it shows, why it is accepted, how to see it, and what
would fix it.

Entries are numbered by the part of Strata that they concern: KI-1 is the
Printer, KI-2 is the label templates, KI-3 is what the project cannot test in
its own environment.


======================================================================
KI-1  THE PRINTER
======================================================================

KI-1.1  The tops of the accents widehat and widetilde touch the line above
        their cell in the equation tables of the Reference Manual

  What:    In the equation tables of the Reference Manual (chapter 9), the
           result column shows the two accents \widehat and \widetilde in
           display size.  The top of each wide accent touches the bottom of
           the line above its cell.
  Where:   The printed Reference Manual, chapter 9, the table of accents.  The
           Editor draws the same equations on the screen without the fault.
  Why:     The accents are drawn by LaTeX.  The fault is in the way that LaTeX
           places a tall accent in a table cell with no space above it, not
           in the text of the manual.  The owner decided on 2026-11-21 to
           accept it: the table stays readable, and the fix would be a
           change of the layout of every equation cell for two commands.
  See it:  Print the Reference Manual to a PDF and look at the table of
           accents in chapter 9 at a large zoom.
  Fix:     A line of space above the cells of that one table.

KI-2  THE LABEL TEMPLATES (docs/LABELS.txt, task 50)
======================================================================

KI-2.1  The geometry comes from the gLabels template data, not from Avery's
        own files

  What:    The size, the margins and the pitch of each sticker sheet come from
           the open template data of the program gLabels, read on 2026-11-21.
           Avery's own product pages give only the size of a sticker and the
           count of stickers on a sheet.  Avery's blank templates are Word
           or PDF files that the research tool could not read, and two of
           Avery's sites (avery.co.uk and Zweckform) refused the tool with
           the status 403.
  Where:   docs/LABELS.txt, every number of every sheet, with its source.
  Why:     No other published source of the margins and the pitch was
           available.  The sheets were printed to PDF and measured: the
           stickers of the PDF lie within 0.036 mm of the data.  That proves
           that the templates follow the data, and does not prove that the
           data follow the real sheet.
  See it:  Print a template to paper and hold the sheet against it.
  Fix:     Read Avery's own template files, or measure a printed sheet.

KI-2.2  The pitch of the sheet 5160 differs by 0.79 mm between two sources

  What:    The pitch that the gLabels data give for the sheet 5160 is 0.79 mm
           different from the pitch in the Microsoft knowledge base article
           Q146892.  The sheets 5197 and 5295 differ in the same way.
  Where:   docs/LABELS.txt, the notes of the three sheets.  The templates use
           the gLabels pitch for every sheet, so that all margins and
           pitches come from one source.
  Why:     Only a printed sheet can say which pitch is right: the project has
           no stock of Avery sheets and does not print on a physical
           printer without the owner's permission.
  See it:  Print the template of the 5160 on paper and lay a real sheet over
           it; the stickers at the foot of the sheet show the difference.
  Fix:     Measure one printed sheet and change the data in
           tests/labeldata.py.

KI-2.3  Seven sheets are not available, because no published number was found

  What:    The sheets 5390, 5392, L7159, L7106, L7418 and 6240 and the spine
           labels of the sheet 5931 have no template.  The sheet L4730 was
           left out because its ten stickers across are more than a block of
           the Printer can wind (nine columns at most).
  Where:   docs/LABELS.txt, the section of the sheets that are missing.
  Why:     The rule of the task is that no number is taken from memory: a sheet
           with no number in a published source is not made.
  Fix:     Find the published numbers and add the sheets to
           tests/labeldata.py; the generator makes the templates.

KI-2.4  Stickers fill down the first column, then the next

  What:    When the text of a label template runs on, it fills the first
           column of stickers from the top to the bottom and then the second
           column.  Avery's own software fills the sheet along the rows: the
           first row from left to right, then the second row.
  Where:   Every label template of Strata.
  Why:     The template is one block of text in winding columns, a layout in
           which the text of a block fills one column and then goes to the
           next.  A winding block has this order and no other.  A layout in
           which the text fills the rows needs a table with a fixed width
           for every cell, and the measurements showed that such a table
           misses the stickers by up to 1.4 mm.
  See it:  Type the numbers one to thirty on a template of the 5160 and
           print it: the number two is below the number one.
  Fix:     A table layout with the cell widths measured for each sheet, or a
           new feature of the Printer that fills the stickers along the rows.

KI-2.5  Text that is too long for a sticker is not cut and not reported

  What:    A sticker holds as many lines as the block has.  A line more than
           the sticker holds is printed, and it pushes the stickers below it
           down the page; the program gives no message.
  Where:   Every label template.
  Why:     The Printer cannot cut a block at a height, and the program does not
           count the lines of a sticker.  The manual says so.
  See it:  Type six lines on a sticker of the 5160, which holds three, and
           print.
  Fix:     A check that counts the lines of each sticker and a message that
           names the sticker.

KI-2.6  Round stickers use the box around the circle, and the hole of a disc
        is not left free

  What:    The template of a round sticker, or of a disc label, has a
           rectangular block of the size of the box that holds the circle.
           Text can fall outside the circle, and on the hole in the middle of
           a CD label.
  Where:   The templates of the sheets 5195, 5193, 5194 and 5931 and the others
           with a round or disc shape.
  Why:     The Printer sets rectangular blocks.  The manual says that the
           writer of the label must keep the text inside the circle.
  Fix:     A block of a shape other than a rectangle, which the Printer does
           not have.

KI-3  WHAT THE PROJECT CANNOT TEST IN ITS OWN ENVIRONMENT
======================================================================

The project builds and tests Strata on a Debian machine with no desktop
session and on a second machine that runs commands over ssh.  The tests use
a pseudo terminal and an X server that has no window manager.  These
statements of the manual are therefore checked against the source of the
program and not by a run.

KI-3.1  The Linux console

  What:    The Linux console is the text screen of the kernel that is shown
           with no terminal program.  The manual says which keys the console
           keeps for itself and which keys reach the program.
  Why:     A pseudo terminal is not a console: the kernel keymap is not in
           the path of its keys.  The project has no machine on which a
           console can be driven by the tests.
  How it is checked:  The reader of keys in term.c takes the forms of the
           console for F1 to F5 and the numeric forms; no code is specific to
           the console.  The rows of the manual that name the keys of the
           kernel keymap are marked not testable here in the ledgers
           (docs/MANUAL-CHECK-LEDGER-README.txt names them).
  Fix:     Drive a real console, for example in a virtual machine with a
           script that sends key codes to it.

KI-3.2  The keys that a user's own terminal, window manager or desktop keep

  What:    Many terminals, window managers and desktops take keys such as
           Alt-F4, Ctrl-Alt-arrows or Alt-Tab before the program sees them.
           The manual has a table of these keys for the terminal and for the
           X11 version.
  Why:     Each table row depends on a program that is not installed in the
           test environment, in a version that changes with the desktop.
  How it is checked:  Against the survey of terminals and desktops in
           claude-interchange/TERMINAL-DOCS-SURVEY.txt and the key map in
           claude-interchange/KEY-MAP.txt, which were made from the
           documentation of each program.  No key was sent to each program.
  Fix:     A matrix of tests that starts each terminal and desktop with a
           script and records which keys arrive.

KI-3.3  A terminal that reports modified keys

  What:    Some terminals send a distinct code for a key with Ctrl, Alt or
           Shift together, for example the Ctrl-Shift forms of the arrow keys.
           The terminal version of the Editor reads these codes.
  Why:     No terminal of that kind is installed in the test environment, and
           the tests cannot make a terminal emulator send them.
  How it is checked:  The reader of keys in term.c was read against the
           documented sequences of these terminals.  No terminal of that kind
           was run.
  Fix:     Run the tests under a terminal emulator that sends the codes.

KI-3.4  The close button of the window of the X11 version

  What:    The manual says that the close button of the window of the X11
           version acts as F12 and Quit, with the choices Save and Discard.
  Why:     The X server of the tests, Xvfb, has no window manager, and a
           window manager draws the close button and sends the close request.
  How it is checked:  The owner confirmed on 2026-11-21 that the close button
           behaves like F12, Quit; the program handles the close request of
           the window in the same way that it handles that command.
  Fix:     A test under a window manager, or a test that sends the close
           request to the window with a protocol message.

KI-3.5  The Control key held with xdotool --window

  What:    A key sent with xdotool --window and the key Ctrl-F5 did not open the
           Paste from Library box, but the same key pressed with a Control key
           that was really held did.  A message box that the held Control key
           opened closed when the key was released.
  Why:     The cause is not known.  It may be a limit of the way that xdotool
           sends a key to a window, which differs from a key from the keyboard.
  How it is checked:  It is not checked: the behaviour is recorded here
           because the test of the key did not give the same result as the
           key of a real keyboard.
  Fix:     Find out in a test that sends the key as the keyboard does.
