MEASUREMENTS: MEMORY, TIME AND DISK OF STRATA
=============================================

Why this file exists.  The owner's review of the prerequisites document (the
comments 7, 20, 21 and 24 of docs/PREREQ-REVIEW.txt) asked that every
statement about memory, speed and disk be measured, with sizes that a writer
understands (pages and words) and not "small".  This file holds the
measurements.  The script that made them is tests/measure.py (a benchmark; the
smoke runner never starts it).

THE PAGE IS 500 WORDS.  The owner (comment 24 on the story sample) found that
a page of the generated test documents was 735 words, about half as large
again as a typed page of 500 words.  Task 42 made every measurement again with
500 words to a page (WORDS_PER_PAGE of tests/measure.py), on a machine that was
quiet, with the sizes in pages of the first measurement.  The tables below
are the new measurement.  The measurement with 735 words to a page is kept at
the end of this file, in the part BASELINE: THE 735 WORDS PER PAGE MEASUREMENT;
its numbers are not comparable with these (another page, another machine).
A size of N pages has about 500 N words.  The Printer lays out about 735 words
on a page of the default layout, so the PDF of N pages of 500 words has about
0.68 N pages; the tables say both ("pages" is always the page of 500 words and
"PDF pages" the pages that the Printer made).

THE SHORT ANSWER (for the prerequisites text)
---------------------------------------------

  - A 100 page report (50,000 words) needs about 5 MB of memory in the
    terminal version and about 14 MB in the X11 version, and about 5 and 15 MB
    after you have moved around it and saved it.
  - A 1,000 page book (500,000 words) needs about 20 MB to open (29 in the
    X11 version) and 28 to 38 MB after scrolling to the end and saving.
  - The largest document that could be made, the whole Mark Twain collection
    (2.6 million words, 5,258 pages of 500 words, 3,625 pages that the Printer
    makes, a 14.8 MB file), needs about 95 MB to open (104 in the X11
    version) and 145 to 155 MB after scrolling to the end, searching and
    saving (217 to 227 MB before the memory work).  It opens in 0.6
    seconds (terminal) or 1.1 seconds (X11), and a search across it takes
    0.5 seconds.
  - The memory is about 6.4 times the size of the file when the document is
    opened, about 8.5 times after the whole document has been laid out, and
    about 9.8 times after a save (6.4, 12.8 and 14.6 times before the memory
    work).  It grows in step with the document: twice the pages need about
    twice the memory.  There is no sign of a blow-up.  A save takes a little
    longer than before (656 ms against 593 ms at 5,258 pages, the build
    before the memory work; see AFTER THE MEMORY REDUCTIONS).
  - Printing to a PDF takes about half a second for a letter and 14 seconds for
    the whole collection (3,625 PDF pages); the Printer and LaTeX need 51 MB
    up to
    2,000 pages and 87 MB for the whole collection.
  - The installed programs, the documents and the templates take 5.4 MB.  The
    manuals as PDF files take 3.7 MB more, and the sample documents 0.1 MB.
  - The programs LaTeX, ghostscript, the spell checker and the thesaurus,
    which a machine may lack, are 126 packages: a download of 191 MB and about
    660 MB installed (Debian, counted from a base system; not counted again).
  - Recommended (derived from these numbers, not measured): for any document
    up to the size of the whole collection, 256 MB of free memory and 700 MB of
    free disk (the prerequisites included) are enough in both versions.  The
    Editor needs at most 155 MB for such a book (the X11 version, after a
    save; the terminal version 145 MB; the print needs 87 MB); for a 1,000
    page book 64 MB is enough.

HOW IT WAS MEASURED
-------------------

The machine: Intel Core i7-6700HQ (4 cores, 8 threads, 2.6 GHz, at most 3.5
GHz by the setting of this machine), 15 GB of memory, Ubuntu 26.04 (Linux 7.0),
release builds of 2026-11-20.  This is NOT the machine of the first measurement
(an AMD Ryzen 7 7840HS): it is slower, so the times are about twice those of the
baseline at the end of this file for the same code.  That is why the builds of
before the memory work and of the memory work only were built and measured
again here (commits 9a329148, cc32d06b3), and the pairs of the tables are
from the same machine and the same hour.  Nothing else ran while the timings
did: before each phase the load average of the minute was below 0.3 (the
script waits for it); during the runs it was 0.30 on the average, which is the
measuring script itself, and at most 0.52 (terminal), 0.48 (X11) and 1.09 (the
Printer, which starts LaTeX).  One leftover process of an earlier job (an
emulator that took about a fifth of a processor) was found and stopped after
the first timing run; every timing was then made again, and the results of the
first run (which differed by less than a tenth) are not used.  The files were
in the memory cache of the system (a cold disk was not measured).

The documents.  They are made from the Mark Twain text of the owner
(twain.txt, which was only read).  A page is 500 words (WORDS_PER_PAGE of
tests/measure.py): a document of N pages holds the first paragraphs that make
500 N words; the word counts below are real.  The Printer makes about 0.68 N
pages of the default layout of such a document (checked: the 1,812,586 word
document of "3625 pages" prints as 2,492 PDF pages, 727 words to a PDF page;
the whole collection, 2,628,948 words, prints as 3,625 PDF pages).  Every size
exists in two forms: a plain document (one block for each paragraph) and a
structured document (a section for every 25 pages).  The one page document is a
letter of 380 words.  The Twain text holds 2,628,948 words that the generator
takes, which is 5,258 pages of 500 words, so the sizes 1 to 3625 pages of the
first measurement all exist, and one size more: the whole collection (5,258
pages, made with the request 5500).  Each document ends with a word that
occurs once; the search looks for it from the start.

The runs.  Every measure is the median of three runs, each in a fresh process.
The memory is the peak resident memory of the process (VmHWM of /proc), read
after each step, so each column includes the ones before it.  The time of a
step is the time from the key until the program has written nothing to the
screen and used no processor time for 0.15 seconds (terminal), or has used no
processor time for 0.4 seconds (X11, in a private virtual display; its times
move in steps of 50 ms).  The steps are: start the program until the first
screen is drawn (open), Ctrl-End, a search from the start to the end for a word
that is found only at the very end, and a save after typing one character.
The Printer was run to a PDF with the error log off; its memory is the largest
of the Printer and of everything it started (pdflatex).

A fault of the script was found and mended on the way: in the X11 run the
search leaves the Replace? prompt on the screen, which took the typed character
and the Ctrl-S, so the save was never made (save_ok was false in every run,
and the save column showed the time of nothing).  The run now presses Escape
after the search; save_ok is true in every run of the tables below.

The three runs agree closely: the memory within 1 MB and the times within
about 10 percent, with rare outliers (one run of the open of the 2000 page
plain document took 301 ms against 234 ms for the structured one).  Below 100
pages the memory of the program itself (3 to 5 MB) is larger than that of the
document.

    the tip of the main line has later changes besides the one-run save, so
    the difference is not charged to the save alone.

    the open and the search take the same time.

    the change.

    vary by about 1 MB from run to run; they are not worth a table row.

PRINTING WITH SEVERAL PASSES (task 32)
--------------------------------------

The earlier prints were plain text, which LaTeX settles in one run.  These
documents (tests/measure.py multipass) are structured documents of the sizes

measurement has all of them already, so its own table was empty.

WHAT WAS NOT MEASURED
---------------------

  - A cold disk (the first start after a boot): the files were cached.
  - Slow computers: only this one.  The memory does not depend on the speed.
  - Documents with pictures or equations: the documents are text.  Pictures
    and equations cost the Printer more time (LaTeX runs for each picture) and
    memory.  The table of contents, the index, the lists, cross references,
    footnotes and a long table were measured in task 32 (PRINTING WITH
    SEVERAL PASSES above).
  - Print and Preview in the window, and the Viewer (dviview): only the
    Printer to a PDF.
  - Documents of more than 5,258 pages (the whole collection), or with a very
    deep or very wide tree of sections
    of sections (the structured documents had chapters of 25 pages).
  - Wayland and a real X server: the X11 version ran in a virtual display
    (Xvfb), which draws in memory; a real display adds the time of the
    graphics driver, and a remote display adds the network (the lag item of
    docs/WORKLIST.txt: a display forwarded with ssh -Y is slow).
  - The painting of the terminal: the times of the terminal version are the
    time until the Editor has written the screen, and not the time that a
    terminal program takes to paint it.

PROBLEMS FOUND
--------------

None.  The memory and the times grow in step with the size of the document,
from 1 to 3,625 pages.  One figure was worth a note: after a save the memory is
about 13 percent above the memory after the search (221 against 196 MB for
3,625 pages).  Task 32 found the cause (the buffer of the save, which grew by
doubling) and reduced it, together with the largest part of the memory, the
copy that Undo keeps of the whole document (docs/MEMORY.txt).

  - Recommended (derived from these numbers, not measured): for any document
    up to the size of a 3,600 page book, 512 MB of free memory and 700 MB of
    free disk (the prerequisites included) are enough in both versions.
    After task 32 the Editor needs at most 153 MB for such a book (the
    X11 version, after a save; the terminal version 145 MB; the print needs
    90 MB), so 256 MB of free memory is enough; for a 1,000 page book 64 MB
    is enough.

HOW IT WAS MEASURED
-------------------

The machine: AMD Ryzen 7 7840HS (16 threads), 15 GB of memory, Debian 13
(Linux 6.12), a release build of 2026-11-18, load average 0.2 at the start,
nothing else running that mattered.  The files were in the memory cache of the
system (a cold disk was not measured).  The times are therefore those of a
fast machine with a warm cache; a slower computer takes longer in proportion
to its processor, and a hard disk adds its read time.

The documents.  They are made from the Mark Twain text in sample.docs (the
owner's file; it was only read).  A page is 735 words: that is what the
Printer makes of this text at the default layout (checked: the 735,110 word
document prints as 1,014 pages).  The word counts below are real, and the
page counts are the pages of the PDF that the Printer made.  Every size exists
in two forms: a plain document (one block for each paragraph) and a structured
document (a section for every 25 pages, about 140 of them in the largest).
The 1 page document is a letter of 380 words.  The text does not hold enough
words for 4,500 pages: the whole file prints as 3,625 pages, so that is the
largest size.  Each document ends with a word that occurs once; the search
looks for it from the start.

The runs.  Every measure is the median of three runs, each in a fresh process.
The memory is the peak resident memory of the process (VmHWM of /proc), read
after each step, so each column includes the ones before it.  The time of a
step is the time from the key until the program has written nothing to the
screen and used no processor time for 0.15 seconds (terminal), or has used no
processor time for 0.4 seconds (X11, in a private virtual display).  The steps
are: start the program until the first screen is drawn (open), Ctrl-End, a
search from the start to the end for a word that is found only at the very
end, and a save after typing one character.  The Printer was run to a PDF
with the error log off; its memory is the largest of the Printer and of
everything it started (pdflatex).

The three runs agree closely: the memory mostly within 2 MB and the times
within about 10 percent, with rare outliers.  Below 100 pages the memory of
the program itself (3 to 5 MB) is larger than that of the document, and the
runs differ by a few megabytes.

What was measured:
  - The memory after a save is 34 percent lower on the largest document
    (220 -> 145 MB) and 35 percent on 1000 pages (64 -> 42 MB); at the end of
    the document it is 34 percent lower (192 -> 126 MB).  The memory at the open
    is the same (the Undo copy is made at the first key).
  - The memory work alone made a save SLOWER: 278 -> 345 ms at 3625 pages (+24
    percent), 86 -> 106 ms at 1000 pages.  The cause: the buffer of a save was
    made exact by a first run over every token of the document that only
    counted the bytes (88 ms at 3625 pages, 25 ms at 1000 pages), and then a
    second run that wrote them (121 ms).  The first run is gone: the buffer now
    starts at an estimate that costs a pass over the blocks and not the tokens,
    the bytes are made in ONE run, and the buffer is cut to the file at the end
    (the part of it that was never written is not resident).  A save now takes
    267 ms at 3625 pages, 82 ms at 1000 pages: a little less than before the
    memory work, and the memory after a save is the same.  The saved files are
    the same bytes (17 of 17 documents).
  - Moving to the end of the document is faster (106 -> 34 ms at 3625 pages);
    the open and the search take the same time.
  - The X11 Editor needs 4 to 8 MB more than the terminal one (3.7 to 8.1 MB
    in all the sizes, plain and structured), before and after the change.
  - Documents of 50 pages and fewer: the figures are between 3 and 7 MB and
    vary by about 2 MB from run to run (the same document gives 2.8 and 4.7 MB
    in two runs of the same build); they are not worth a table row.

WHAT WAS NOT MEASURED
---------------------

  - A cold disk (the first start after a boot): the files were cached.
  - Slow computers: only this one.  The memory does not depend on the speed.
  - Documents with pictures or equations: the documents are text.  Pictures
    and equations cost the Printer more time (LaTeX runs for each picture) and
    memory.  The table of contents, the index, the lists, cross references,
    footnotes and a long table were measured in task 32 (PRINTING WITH
    SEVERAL PASSES above).
  - Print and Preview in the window, and the Viewer (dviview): only the
    Printer to a PDF.
  - Documents of more than 3,625 pages, or with a very deep or very wide tree
    of sections (the structured documents had chapters of 25 pages).
  - Wayland and a real X server: the X11 version ran in a virtual display
    (Xvfb), which draws in memory; a real display adds the time of the
    graphics driver, and a remote display adds the network (the lag item of
    docs/WORKLIST.txt: a display forwarded with ssh -Y is slow).
  - The painting of the terminal: the times of the terminal version are the
    time until the Editor has written the screen, and not the time that a
    terminal program takes to paint it.

PROBLEMS FOUND
--------------

None.  The memory and the times grow in step with the size of the document,
from 1 to 3,625 pages.  One figure was worth a note: after a save the memory is
about 13 percent above the memory after the search (221 against 196 MB for
3,625 pages).  Task 32 found the cause (the buffer of the save, which grew by
doubling) and reduced it, together with the largest part of the memory, the
copy that Undo keeps of the whole document (docs/MEMORY.txt).
